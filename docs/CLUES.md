# Every K4 clue we have (as of 2026-11-15)

One page of everything known *for* K4, as opposed to `negatives/NEGATIVES.md` (what it is *not*).
Confidence: **fact** (checked against the ciphertext), **stated** (Sanborn/Scheidt on record), **reported**
(credible secondary source), **ours** (our own measurement, with its caveat). Sources are in
`research_notes/` and `negatives/community.jsonl`.

## 1. The text itself (fact)

* 97 letters: `OBKRUOXOGHULBSOLIFBBWFLRVQQPRNGKSSOTWTQSJQSSEKZZWATJKLUDIAWINFBNYPVTTMZFPKWGDKZXTJCDIGKUHUAUEKCAR`
* Carved as **4 + 31 + 31 + 31** letters: the end of the K3 row after the "?", then three full rows (`data/sculpture_layout.json`).
  Row starts are at positions 1, 5, 36 and 67. EASTNORTHEAST sits wholly inside row 2; BERLINCLOCK straddles rows 3 and 4 (BER | LINCLOCK).
* All 26 letters appear. Index of coincidence **0.0361**: flat, like random text.
* The clues need **3 E's**, but the ciphertext has only 2, so pure transposition is impossible.
* Two letters encrypt to themselves: **S→S at 33** and **K→K at 74**, so this is not Enigma-like.
* **R→P twice** (28 and 66, 38 apart). **T→V** (25) and **L→V** (67) sit 42 apart, as do A→K (32) and K→K (74), so no cipher whose substitution repeats every 7, 14, 21 or 42 letters.
* **N→Q and O→Q are adjacent** (26–27), so it isn't one substitution per carved row.
* Doubled letters at 19 (BB), 26 (QQ), 33 (SS), 43 (SS), 47 (ZZ), 68 (TT); five of the six fall on the same position mod 7.
* Five W's (21, 37, 49, 59, 75); Y and M appear once each.

## 3. What Sanborn has said (stated)

* **1990:** The lower half uses **two separate systems** of encipherment, "and that is a major clue in itself".
* **2005:** He used **"matrix codes Ed gave me"** plus his own **"visual systems"**, modified so that Scheidt couldn't decipher it. He left **instructions in the earlier text that refer to later text**. His work is **"rife with mistakes on purpose"**.
* **2006** (while correcting his K2 error): the **answers to the first three passages contain clues to the fourth**. K2's ending is "WEST X **LAYER TWO**", not "ID BY ROWS".
* **2010–2020:** Released the four clues above (Nov 2010, Nov 2014, 29 Jan 2020, Aug 2020). With CLOCK (2014): "You'd better **delve into that particular clock**", but "there are several really interesting clocks in Berlin". 2025: confirmed the plaintext's clock is the **World Clock**.
* **2019:** He doesn't care what method a solver uses. You don't need a particular book, "just to be able to read".
* **Nov 2025 (final clues):**
  * His **1986 trip to Egypt** and the **1989 fall of the Berlin Wall** figure in the solution.
  * BERLINCLOCK is the **World Clock (Weltzeituhr) at Alexanderplatz**.
  * The theme is **"delivering a message"**.
  * **K5** (97 letters, made at the same time as K4) shares coded words with K4 in the same positions, and links to K2's "it's buried out there somewhere".
  * **"Who says it is even a math solution?"**
* **The plaintext is a riddle;** solving K4 is not the end.

## 4. What Scheidt has said (stated)

* The four sections use four processes: "two of them are similar and the other two are different things".
* In K4 he **"masked the English language"**. You must "**solve the technique first** and then go for the puzzle", and "the masking technique may not be known".
* There was a **"change in the methodology"** from K1–K3. K4 is in English and "more than one stage".
* "I could have moved to a **different math base**" (bases 2 to 16, a Mayan base-60 example). He taught **"shifting matrices"**.
* He rates the difficulty about **9 out of 10**. He asked the CIA whether he could use an extinct language and was told no.

## 5. The plaintext strips and the coding system (reported)

* In Sanborn's Smithsonian papers (found Sep 2025) the K4 plaintext survives as **five pages of 1990 sentence strips, cut up and taped out of order**.
* Sanborn's own account: for the CIA's Department of Historical Intelligence he **cut the plaintext into sentences and handed them over mixed up**, so officials could check the content without memorising it.
* The RR Auction lot (sold Nov 2025 for $962,500, to Paradigm) contained:
  * K4's **"original coding system"** and **"coding charts"**;
  * **two handwritten plaintexts used for "screen cutting"** (the copper letters were cut from a screen layout);
  * a **1988 alternate K4 plaintext and chart, "now known as K5"**.
* Kobek and Byrne: the plaintext is **exactly 97 letters, makes semantic sense, and has the clues at the stated positions**. They don't have the method.
* **What this implies:**
  * The plaintext is **several sentences**.
  * It was **fixed before 1990**.
  * The encryption ran off **paper charts** (a hand process).
  * The cut and copper layout was planned from handwritten sheets.
  * K1–K3 mark sentence breaks with **X** (K2: "FIELD X THE INFORMATION"; K3: "MIST X CAN YOU SEE"), so K4's sentences may be separated the same way.

## 6. Clues in K1–K3 and the sculpture (fact / stated)

* **Keys:** K1 PALIMPSEST and K2 ABSCISSA, both on the KRYPTOS alphabet. K3 is a double grid rotation (widths 24 and 8). "PALIMPSEST and ABSCISSA" can be anagrammed to "P.S. It's as simple as ABC".
* **Deliberate misspellings:** IQLUSION (L→Q), UNDERGRUUND (O→U), DESPARATLY (E→A), and PALIMPCEST on the tableau (S→C). K2 dropped an S ("ID BY ROWS" → "LAYER TWO").
* **Raised letters "YAR"** in K3, and an extra **L** on the tableau, which makes HILL readable down the right edge.
* **Themes:**
  * light and shadow (K1);
  * the earth's magnetic field, a buried location, coordinates near CIA HQ, and WW = William Webster (K2);
  * Carter opening Tutankhamun's tomb (K3).
* **The site:** a lodestone, a compass rose pointing roughly east-northeast, Morse code slabs, a reflecting pool, petrified wood.
* **Sanborn's other works:** the **Cyrillic Projector** (solved 2003) used a keyed Vigenère (mixed alphabet ТЕНЬ = "shadow", key МЕДУЗА) with the **key restarting at each block**. Sanborn says K4 is "a very different animal" in theme.

## 7. Statistical oddities (ours / Bean / Stehle)

* **Bean (2021):**
  * the known plaintext and cipher letters sit unusually close in the alphabet (about 1 in 5,520);
  * 11 repeated pairs at width 21 (about 1 in 6,750 raw, about 1 in 90–210 after correcting for picking the best width).
* **Under plain A–Z Vigenère,** 10 of the 13 EASTNORTHEAST key values are within ±3. The BERLINCLOCK key values are large.
* **KRYPTOS letters (K, R, Y, P, T, O, S) as plaintext** encrypt to within ±2 of themselves in 9 of 10 cases (Scott; post hoc).
* **Ours:**
  * Stehle's lag-4 run (DIAWINFBN) **p = 0.003** and the mod-7 doublets **p = 0.001**, against 200,000 shuffles (post hoc caveat).
  * **No** periodic signature in the letter differences (p = 0.16).
  * The clues force a key jump of 12–13 alphabet steps somewhere, so the key is not a slowly turning dial.
* **Ours: fingerprints.** The hand-made fingerprint ranking, the neural detective and Bean all point to **direct substitution, plain or near-plain alphabet, a structured key that isn't simply periodic, no transposition**.

## 8. What all of that rules out at the family level (summary; details in NEGATIVES.md)

* **Dead:**
  * repeating keys of length 1–26 (and 27–29 for standard and keyword alphabets);
  * autokey;
  * running keys from known texts;
  * Quagmire I/II/III with keyword alphabets;
  * transposition plus most key models;
  * Gromark with hidden alphabets (weakly);
  * World Clock 1989 keys;
  * Egypt-era alphabets;
  * teleprinter ciphers;
  * self-masking;
  * strips cut at carved rows or fixed widths.
* **Open:**
  * two stacked keys at long lengths (a search problem; round 6 is attacking it);
  * hidden-alphabet keys of length 23 or more;
  * key schedules tied to **unknown boundaries such as sentences**;
  * genuinely non-arithmetic "visual" steps.
