# Idea 108: the strip is moved by a text (key = running sum of a sentence or a sculpture reading)

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No known text fits, and the best "English" moving-text fragment for K4 is noise. K4
looks like a shuffled K4 in all three parts.

## The idea

Round 10's sliding key: each key letter says how far to move the strip, so the key in force is the running sum of
the key letters. It was only tried with a repeating word of up to 17 letters. Here the moving text is a sentence or
a reading of the sculpture, so the key never repeats. On any given tableau the clues fix the key values at 21-33 and
63-73, and their differences are the moving text itself: 12 letters under EASTNORTHEAST, 10 under BERLINCLOCK, 42
places apart. Attacks 57 and 58 compared the key letters with text, never the differences.

## What was run

`attacks/108_strip_moved_by_text.py`, helpers `kryptos/idea108_moving.py`, test `tests/test_idea_108.py`. 13 minutes.

* **Settings:** 666,993 alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS, dictionary keyword alphabets and their two transposition-mixed
  forms) x 5 layouts x Vigenere/Beaufort x strip moved forward or backward (this covers variant Beaufort and the
  reversed table) x text numbered on A-Z, KRYPTOS or the tableau alphabet, from 0 or from 1: 80,039,064 settings.
* **Part A, known texts, exact:** the 22 letters must stand in one of 198,934 readings (attack 58's: both panels,
  K1-K3 plaintext, Morse; rows, columns, diagonals, back and forth, reversed; every d-th letter up to d = 60;
  9.86M letters), block 2 exactly 42 letters after block 1; also with the gap free inside one reading.
* **Part B, unknown English:** the 22 letters scored on the big model (16 quadgrams), best 200 kept.
* **Part C:** second differences (the text moves a strip that moves the strip), 11 + 9 letters, known texts only.
* The clue pre-filter passes the encryptor (no fact or firm statement broken).

## Scope limits

* Parts A and C are solid for the listed tableaux, numberings and readings. The "best of 22" figures are only a
  gauge: a setting is placed when three six-letter pieces of its fragments occur in the texts, so partial matches
  without six letters in a row are not counted. A full match is always placed.
* Part B is thin, as idea 57 warned. Noise over 80M settings tops out at -4.46 to -4.74, the planted English
  fragments scored -4.03 to -4.61, and the first planted answer won by 0.001 (-4.614 against -4.615). An English
  moving text whose 22 letters are not clearly English (below about -4.5) would be missed. Part B excludes only the
  clear cases.
* Not covered: hidden or non-dictionary alphabets, other numberings of the text, German or other-language texts,
  a text that restarts between the blocks, third differences.

## Surprising

Nothing in K4. The run is cheap (bit map of every six-letter piece of the texts, 1.8% full), so more texts can be
added at almost no cost. The one useful lesson: 22 letters are too few to judge an unknown English moving text;
the follow-up is to grow the moving text and the plaintext together outward from the clue blocks.
