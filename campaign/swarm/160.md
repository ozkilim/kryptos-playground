# Idea 160: find the letter on the copper, write its neighbour

**Verdict: dead-in-scope.** K4 sits inside the shuffled controls on every count.

## The idea

A hand code with no arithmetic. Keep a finger on a carved panel. Move on to the next place where a letter
occurs and take the letter at a fixed offset from it (beside it, above it, or one step east-north-east:
right, up, right). Ideas 85 and 106 used K4 letters as counts; this one searches for the letter itself.

It can be read two ways, and both were run:

* **Forward:** the decoder searches for the K4 letter and reads the neighbour. One start cell gives one
  full 97-letter reading.
* **Reverse:** the encoder searches for the plain letter and carves the neighbour. Then the walk over the
  clue words is fixed by the known plain letters, so each clue word is tested exactly from every pointer.

## What was run

`attacks/160_find_letter_write_neighbour.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea160_neighbour.py`).

* 50 texts: cipher panel (K1-K3 cells only; all 28 rows), K1-K3 plaintext written into the carved cells
  (alone; followed by K4), tableau panel (28 rows; 26-row body), Morse text. Left- or right-aligned. Read by
  rows, by columns, and both backwards.
* 30 offsets: 1-3 cells left/right, 1-3 rows up/down, 8 knight steps (east-north-east is -1 row, +2
  cells), 4 diagonals, 1-3 places along the reading order. Edge cells either skipped or left as a hole.
* 5 pointer rules: same cell allowed, next, one-on, reset at each carved K4 row, jump to the neighbour.
* Every start cell. 11,451,060 settings per role, 22.9 million in all.

## Numbers

| | K4 | 40 shuffled K4s |
|---|---|---|
| Forward: best clue letters in place (24 needed) | 8 | 6-9 (7 of 40 at or above 8) |
| Forward: best English, big model (needs -5.1) | -5.96 | -6.30 to -5.62 (12 of 40 at or above) |
| Reverse: best clue letters (24 needed) | 9 | 7-9 (15 of 40 at 9) |
| BERLIN / NORTHEAST anywhere in a reading | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |

The 11.45 million forward settings give only 515,953 different readings, because all starts that reach the
same first cell walk the same path. The best K4 reading (8/24) is gibberish: -7.08, word cover 0.09.

**Planted: 7/7.** Four forward messages were found at 24/24 at the exact planted setting and read back
97/97, including the idea's own case (cipher panel, offset (-1,+2), random start). Three reverse messages
passed the exact clue-word test and decoded at 96, 96 and 97 of 97 letters.

## Surprises

* **A trap.** With K4 on the reference panel, the finger simply walks along K4 itself and reads the row
  three above, which is K3's plaintext ("...USED THE FLAME TO FLICKER BUT PRESENTLY DETAILS OF THE ROOM...",
  -4.51). It looks like a hit and means nothing: a shuffled K4 placed in the same cells does the same. Such
  straight copies (8+ letters in a row from a panel) are set aside before ranking: 695 for K4, 142-1075 for
  the controls.
* **The forward role is not usable by hand.** The plan asked to show the encoder can always choose an
  occurrence. It cannot. On the cipher panel 0 of 200 ordinary English messages can be written; half get
  stuck within 10-11 letters, the longest run is 41. On the K1-K3 plaintext 0-2 of 200. Only the tableau
  panel allows it (30-138 of 200). The forward plants on the first two texts therefore use strings of common
  words picked so the encoder never gets stuck.
* **The reverse role is not flat enough.** It always encodes, but the index of coincidence comes out at
  0.050-0.054 on the cipher panel (lowest 0.038 in 800 messages) and 0.064-0.067 on plaintext or tableau.
  K4 has 0.0361. So the claim "as flat as the K1/K2 ciphertext it is read from" does not hold.

## Scope limits

Panels as transcribed, left- or right-aligned only (the real letter spacing is uneven). One fixed offset
per message. Search always forwards along the reading order. Not covered: offsets that change per letter or
row, finding the letter on one panel and reading the other panel, letter pairs, corrected K2, any second
layer. The reverse role was bounded on the clue words with free pointers; no K4 setting reached the full
decode. Clue positions taken as exact.
