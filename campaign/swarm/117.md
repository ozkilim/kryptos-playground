# Idea 117: which fed-key models clash on every possible tableau

**Verdict: dead in scope.** 91 of 200 autokey models are impossible on every tableau, hidden ones included.
Every running key that survives that test, and every cipher autokey, has no solution on one hidden alphabet.
Nothing is left to read. K4's numbers are ordinary next to 50 shuffled K4s.

## The idea

A tableau whose rows are all different and whose columns are all different is a Latin square. On any such
square, two letters that share two of (key, plain, cipher) must also share the third. This test needs no
alphabet, so wherever the key letter at a clue position is itself a known letter, it decides the model for
every tableau at once. Idea 94 used it once, by hand, for word autokey. Here it is applied to every fed-key
model whose key letters inside the clues are known.

## What was run

`attacks/117_clash_on_every_tableau.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea117_clash.py`, test in
`tests/test_idea_117.py`. The run took 765 s.

* **Part A (any tableau):**
  * plain autokey at lags 1-60 and cipher autokey at lags 1-68, back and ahead (rows of width w with "the
    letter above" are the same as lag w);
  * idea 74's lag patterns, periods 2-7 (plain lags 1-8, cipher lags 1-20), counted exactly with a small
    constraint solver (cipher p=7 sampled);
  * running keys from every attack-58 reading of the sculpture (every d-th letter, d=1-60) and from the K1-K3
    plaintexts forwards and reversed, at every offset (a bitmask scan over offsets);
  * the second clue also moved by -3 to +3.
* **Part B:** one hidden alphabet on all three sides, X[C] = ±X[P] ± X[K] + E. The systems are solved mod 26
  and need all letters in different places. The plain-autokey chains of idea 100 (lags 15-26) drop the
  unknown middle letter.
* **Part C:** the key letter is read as a number on A-Z or KRYPTOS, with one hidden alphabet for plain and
  cipher.

## Numbers (true clue placement)

| | K4 | 50 shuffled K4s |
|---|---|---|
| fixed models impossible on every tableau (of 200) | **91** (plain 12, cipher 79) | 40-129, median 62 |
| sculpture running-key offsets surviving (of 2.76M) | 45,810 (1.7%) | 26,295-1,242,169, median 945,625 |
| cipher lag patterns surviving, p=2 (of 400) | 9 | 0-211, median 136 |
| Part B: cipher-autokey systems with solutions | 0 | 0-10, median 1 |
| Part B/C: running-key systems with solutions | 0 of 183,484 | 0 in every control |

* **Dead on every tableau:**
  * plain autokey at lags 1-4 back and 1-6 ahead. The two EASTs carry the same key letters but differ in
    cipher: A keyed by E gives L at 22 and K at 31.
  * plain autokey at lag 42, back and ahead;
  * every cipher autokey below lag 30 except 12 back;
  * a scatter of longer cipher lags (listed in the card).

  37 of the 91 kills survive any single wrong clue letter. The other 54 are fragile.
* **Why K4 kills so many running keys:** letters 27 and 65 both have plain R and cipher P. So on any tableau
  the key letters 38 apart there must be equal. 15 of the 50 controls also have such a pair, and their
  counts drop the same way. So this is not a signal.
* **Chains at lags 19-22:** 18 systems impossible, 0 with solutions, 14 undecided.
* **Part C:** 6 cipher-autokey systems have solutions. None decrypts 10 or more quadgrams outside the clues.
* **Moved second clue:** shifts of -3 to +3 give 63-91 models impossible, with controls at a median of 58-64.

**Planted answers:**

* **Latin squares: 6/6.** The models were plain autokey lag 3, cipher autokey lag 11 and the K2 plaintext
  as running key, each on two random Latin squares that are not group tables. The true model never clashes,
  while 82-133 other models and 244-316 of 316 other K2 offsets do.
* **Hidden alphabet:** the true alphabet was listed 8/8 for cipher lag 11 and K2 running key. For plain lag
  3 it was kept 4/4: it satisfies the equations, but 18 equations are too few to list it.

**Pre-filter:** FLAG on the K5 clue, as for every key that follows the message. It was built anyway because
the test is exact and covers hidden tableaux, which no earlier autokey test did.

## Scope limits

* Only keys whose letters inside the clues are known letters of K4 or of a named text. Word or number keys
  are outside the test.
* The key must pick the tableau row by a fixed one-to-one rule.
* Survivors are undecided, not supported. These include:
  * plain lags 5-12 back, 7-12 ahead and 30-52;
  * plain lags 13-29, which have no known triple at all;
  * cipher lags 12 back and 30-68;
  * most lag patterns.
* Parts B and C use one alphabet with ± forms, no multiplier. Their controls were run at the true placement
  only, and the sculpture readings on a sample of 3,000 survivors for 5 controls.
* The 482.9 billion "tested" is mostly lag patterns: per ciphertext and placement, 1.35e9 cipher-feed
  patterns, summed over K4, 50 controls and 7 placements.

## Worth knowing

The R/P pair at 27 and 65 is a one-line constraint on any key source and any tableau: the key letter must
repeat 38 places apart. It is a cheap gate for every future key list.
