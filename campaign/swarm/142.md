# Idea 142: plain autokey 9-14 back or ahead, with a short repeating word on top

**Verdict: dead-in-scope.** K4 gives nothing, the shuffled K4s give nothing, all six planted answers are found.

## The idea

Scheidt called the method simple, memorable and built on key word/s. A remembered word added to the message itself,
read a worksheet-like distance back, fits that:

    key[i] = sign * number(plaintext letter L back, or L ahead) + word[i mod p]

Idea 100 closed the constant case (p = 1) at lags 9-33. Idea 90 added a word of 2-4 but only at lags 7, 8 and 34-48.
Lags 9-14 with a word were open.

## What was run

`attacks/142_autokey_lag_9_14_with_word.py`, helper `kryptos/idea142_wordchain.py`.

* Two clue letters a whole number of lags apart are joined by a chain of 1-5 steps through unknown letters. With the
  tableau known, each chain is one exact equation in the word letters. Lags 9-14 have 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 11 chains,
  so a word of 2-4 leaves 7 to 13 exact checks.
* The word is solved, not tried: one-step chains give a letter at once, two three-step chains with the same letters
  are matched by a hash join, the rest are checks. The solver lists every word that fits, so the test is exact.
* 666,993 alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS, dictionary keyword alphabets and both transposition-mixed forms) x 5 layouts x
  Vigenere/Beaufort x fed letter numbered in M/A-Z/KRYPTOS x sign x back/ahead x lag 9-14 x every word of 2, 3, 4
  letters: **228,220,939,894,272 settings**. The full run took about 16 minutes (931 s) on 40 cores.
* Chains are built from text positions (class = position mod lag), so the run-break mistake idea 105 found in attack
  90 (splitting on the clue index) cannot occur; a test checks this.

Pre-filter: FLAG on the K5 clue (a key that follows the message does not put the same words in the same places),
as for idea 100. Built anyway because the test is exact and the gap was real.

## Scope limits

* Lags 9-14 only; words of 2-4 only; plain feed only; dictionary keyword tableaux only (no hidden alphabets).
* Lags 15+ with a word, and words of 5+ at lags 9-12, are not run (see follow-ups on the card).
* Controls ran on a third of the alphabets each.
* Clue positions are taken as exact.

## Surprise

In some views the clues cannot tell the word's letters apart. With Beaufort and the fed letter numbered like the
plaintext, a three-step chain depends only on the sum of its three word letters, so at lags 13 and 14 a word of 3
comes as a family of 676 words that all close every chain (and differ in the letters between the clues). The solver
lists the whole family; a real pass there would have to be picked out by reading. No such family passed for K4 or
the controls.
