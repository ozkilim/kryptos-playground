# Idea 86: keywords outside the dictionary, solved from the alphabetical tail

**Verdict: dead in scope.** Where the clues decide, no keyword alphabet fits K4. Where alphabets do fit (long keys
only), 1.53 million of them were decrypted and none reads as English. The shuffled K4s look the same, and all 12
planted answers were found and read back.

## The idea

Scheidt: K4 runs "with the correct key word/s", and K1's keyword was a misspelling (PALIMPCEST). Every keyword-tableau
test so far took its alphabets from about 220,000 dictionary words. A name, a German word or a misspelling was never
tried. But a keyword alphabet is a small unknown: m keyword letters in some order, then the other letters in A-Z
order. A letter's place is its keyword slot, or `m + its number - (keyword letters before it)`. So each clue test is
a linear equation mod 26 in a few letter places, and all keyword alphabets that satisfy the equations can be found
exactly, with no word list. The clue pre-filter gives PASS.

## What was run

`attacks/86_keyword_tail.py` (solver in `kryptos/idea86_tail.py`). The solver places the letters that occur in the
equations one at a time, each as "keyword slot s" or "tail, with c keyword letters before it", and forces the last
letter of every equation. Letters in no equation are counted, not searched. It was checked against brute force over
every keyword of 1-3 letters, and every listed solution is re-decrypted the plain way.

* **520 systems per text:** 5 layouts (M/M = Quagmire III; M/AZ, M/KR = Quagmire I; AZ/M, KR/M = Quagmire II) x
  Vigenere or Beaufort form x repeating key of length 1-26 or sliding key of period 1-26.
* **Keyword length m** climbs 1, 2, ... 25 until the system has used 6 million search steps. Level m includes all
  shorter keywords, and m = 25 is every alphabet there is. 433 of K4's 520 systems reached m = 25; the lowest stop
  was m = 8 (the plan asked for 9: six Quagmire III sliding-key systems stopped at 8).
* **Reading.** While a system has at most 50,000 solutions, each is decrypted wherever the clues force the key and
  scored on the big English model outside the clue words. English needs about -5.1; planted texts read -4.3 to -4.9.

K4 is closed more often than the chance figure says, and a little more often than the shuffled K4s, which have
solutions at 14, 18, 20-22 and 25 where K4 has none. A likely reason (not tested) is K4's repeated clue pairs and
fixed points (S->S, K->K, R->P twice), which a shuffle destroys. In the Vigenere form the reason is plain: a fixed
point pins its key letter to zero. That form is empty at all 26 lengths, as the community search found.

Planted answers: **12/12**. SANBORN, SCHEIDT and the random string ZYARKX as keywords at key lengths 13, 19 and 23
(Quagmire III Beaufort), PALIMPCEST with a sliding key of period 7, SCHEIDT on M/KR at 19, WELTZEITUHR on AZ/M at 13.
The true alphabet was listed every time and its reading ranked first of up to 29,654 solutions (-4.26 to -4.92).

## Scope limits

* One unknown keyword alphabet of the plain kind. Not two unknown keywords (Quagmire IV), not transposition-mixed
  alphabets, no key rule other than repeating or sliding, no gap slack.
* **Not decided** beyond the stated keyword length, because more than 50,000 alphabets pass and were not read:
  repeating key 26 (read to 3-5 letters), repeating 24 on M/KR (to 8), sliding 24 (to 7-8), sliding 25 (to 5-7),
  sliding 26 (to 3), Quagmire III sliding 16 and 18 (to 8).
* 523,768 of the solutions read force fewer than 20 quadgrams outside the clues (mostly long sliding keys), so
  English cannot judge them. They are counted but prove nothing either way.
* The Quagmire III sliding-key systems stopped at m = 8-16 (budget), except periods 3, 9, 26 and Beaufort 19.

## Anything surprising

* The search is cheap. The plan expected to stop near m = 9; most systems went to m = 25 in the 15-minute run.
* Quagmire III Beaufort at length 13 has no solution for any alphabet. The community's arbitrary-alphabet search
  listed 13 and 18 as unresolved. This run agrees with it everywhere else (empty at 1-12, 14-17, 19-22, 24, 25;
  satisfiable at 23 and 26), which is a useful check on the solver. Length 18 is closed here only up to 16 letters.
* Several alphabets often give the same reading (they differ only in letters the text never uses), so counts of
  solutions overstate the number of different candidates.
