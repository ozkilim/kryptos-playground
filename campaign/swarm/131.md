# Idea 131: Quagmire IV with two keyword alphabets that need not be words

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No pair of keyword alphabets fits K4 in any cell where chance would give
fewer than one fit. The few chance fits in the looser cells read as noise. The controls look the same,
and all 4 planted answers were found and ranked first.

## The idea

Scheidt said K4 runs "with the correct key word/s". In a Quagmire IV that suggests two keyword
alphabets, one for the plain side and one for the cipher side. The keywords need not be words (K1 used
the misspelling PALIMPCEST). Idea 113 searched Quagmire IV at key length 8 with both alphabets fully
free and had no power. Idea 86 solved one keyword alphabet exactly from its A-Z tail. This idea does
the same for both alphabets at once.

## What was run

`attacks/131_quagmire4_two_keyword_tails.py`, solver `kryptos/idea131_two.py`, test
`tests/test_idea_131.py`. The solver is checked against brute force. The clue pre-filter gives PASS
(score 1.0).

* **Model.** Each side is a keyword of 1-9 different letters followed by the other letters in A-Z order
  ("kw"), or that alphabet written in rows of m and read off by columns ("cols"). The key is repeating,
  with length 1-16, in Vigenere form (c - p = k) or Beaufort form (c + p = k).
* **Cells.** The expected number of chance solutions in a cell is P(26,m1) · P(26,m2) / 26^equations.
  Every cell under 1 was solved to the end: 1,744 cells per text. Cells from 1 to 100 were solved with
  a budget of 10 million steps: 396 cells.
* **Reading.** Every solution was completed and decrypted, then scored on the big model outside the
  clues. English needs about -5.1.
* **Controls.** Plain shuffles of K4 nearly always break at once: the same cipher letter falls in the
  same key slot over two different plain letters. That makes them no test. So each control was redrawn
  until it had no such clash at any key length where K4 has none (8, 11, 12, 13, 16), plus idea 113's
  loop structure at length 8. Only 3 of 26,899 shuffles qualify.
* **Run time.** 6.6 minutes on 14 cores.

| **K4** | **0** | 14 (lengths 13, 16) | -7.95 |

| shuffled 29897 | 0 | 193 | -7.97 |
| shuffled 33883 | 1,728 (length 10) | 788 | -7.41 |

Every length from 1 to 10, and 14 and 15, has no pair at all for K4, even in the extended cells. At key
length 8, all decisive cells hold 0 pairs. These include keywords of 9+7, 8+8 and 7+9 letters.

**Planted: 4/4.** Two cases used random non-word keywords, KLQCMJ and AMDLSTXF, at key 8: one with both
alphabets plain, one with the plain side by columns. Two used SANBORN with a misspelled KRYPTOS
(KRIPTOS) at key 11: one with both plain, one with the cipher side by columns. Each was the best reading
of all readings on its text and read back 97/97 (-4.27 to -4.93).

## Scope limits

* **Keyed-column form not covered.** In that form the columns are read in the alphabetical order of the
  keyword. It depends on the keyword's letter order, which the solver doesn't track.
* **Some cells undecided.** At key 8, the 8+9 and 9+9 letter cells used up their step budget (1.6 and
  28 expected chance pairs). They are not decided.
* **Other limits.** Repeating key only, lengths up to 16. Keywords of at most 9 letters. Exact clue
  positions.

## Surprises

* **Cheap search.** The two-sided search is much cheaper than idea 113's free search: the hardest
  decisive cell took 33 seconds.
* **K4 is closed more often than chance predicts.** K4 has no pair at all at lengths 1-10, 14 and 15,
  even in cells where chance predicts up to 100. The K4-like controls do find chance pairs at 10-12.
