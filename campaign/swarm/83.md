# Idea 83: the S,S and R,R repeats against any state machine

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No signal.

## The idea

Idea 75 killed Wheatstone's clock with the repeated cipher letters inside the clues. This asks how far that goes.
Take any device with a hidden state that moves on after each letter by a hidden amount `g(letter)` mod m, and a
hidden table F that turns a value into the cipher letter:

* moore: `C = F(state)`, `state += g(plain)`
* mealy: `C = F(state + h(plain))`, `state += g(plain)` (a second hidden table h)
* cipher-fed: as mealy, but `state += g(cipher)`

## What came out

The answer is one line and needs no modulus. In the plain-fed devices the step from one F-input to the next
depends only on the two plain letters. So with a one-to-one F a plain pair gives a doubled cipher letter always
or never. The clues hold S,T twice: at letters 32-33 it gives **S,S**, at 23-24 it gives **R,V**. That is
impossible for every m, every table, and any group in place of Z_m. Wheatstone's clock with any two ring sizes
is a special case, so idea 75's result (rings of 27-38 cells) extends to all rings. The R,R repeat is not needed.

## What was run

`attacks/83_repeats_any_state_machine.py`, 73.4M tests in all.

| Model | K4 | 200 shuffled K4s |
|---|---|---|
| moore, equations only | fits 59 of 59 moduli (all steps zero: says nothing) | - |
| moore, F one-to-one | **0 of 59** | none fits 127, some fits 19, undecided 54 |
| moore, one-to-one + different letters move differently | **0 of 59** | 128 / 21 / 51 |
| mealy, F one-to-one | **0 of 59** | 55 / 105 / 40 |
| cipher-fed, F one-to-one | fits 47 of 59 (every m from 14 to 60) | 5 / 181 / 14 |
| moore or mealy, F many-to-one (m = 27..60) | fits 34 of 34 | 34 of 34 on each of 3 shuffles |

* The doubled-pair rule is broken by 46 of the 200 shuffled K4s (23%). K4 breaking it is ordinary, not a clue.
* Known alphabets, only `g` hidden: 666,994 dictionary keyword alphabets (plain and transposition-mixed) x 5
  layouts x Vigenere/Beaufort x 4 feeds (plain or cipher letter, before or including the current letter). The
  key must move by the same amount after the same letter: 10 exact checks plain-fed, 8 cipher-fed.
  K4: **0 passes of 26,679,752**. Three shuffled K4s: 0 of 20.0M, 13.3M and 13.3M judged.
* Planted: **9/9**. Four devices with random tables found at their modulus (moore 26, mealy 26 and 29,
  cipher-fed 26); four known-alphabet drifts found with the right alphabet and view; the rule held on all 1,500
  texts from random one-to-one devices (moore, mealy, clocks with random rings) and failed on 375 of 500
  many-to-one devices, as it should.

## Scope limits

* Dead only when the feed is the **plain** letter and F is **one-to-one**.
* A many-to-one F (several values show the same letter, m > 26) fits every modulus. It is not excluded.
* Cipher-fed with both tables hidden fits every m >= 14: undecidable from 24 letters. Only its
  dictionary-alphabet form was tested.
* Steps that also depend on position, a key word, or more than the last letter are not covered.
* The solver checked Z_m for m = 2..60; the one-line argument covers everything else.
* "Undecided" in the controls means the search hit its node budget (4 x 100,000 per modulus). K4 had none in the
  plain-fed one-to-one models.
* The clue pre-filter FLAGs this family (a message-fed key does not explain K5's shared words). It was built
  anyway because the test is exact and takes minutes.

## Surprising

* The shorter proof was sitting inside idea 75: S,T doubled once and not doubled once kills the clock for
  every ring size at once, without counting turns.
* The moore device is so tight that 127 of 200 random texts fail it at every modulus; EAST appearing twice
  does most of that.
