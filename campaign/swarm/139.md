# Idea 139: four-square and two-square on 26/27-cell rectangles, all squares free

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No square arrangement at all fits ordinary pairs on 2x13/13x2, and nothing fits
w = 7. Four-square stays possible on 3x9/9x3 at one block start, but that is a capacity result, not a reading.

## The idea

Idea 103 tested four-square only with plain squares A-Z or KRYPTOS. With all four squares free, each clue pair
(a, b) -> (x, y) only says: x sits in a's row and b's column, y in b's row and a's column. So "can ANY squares
give these clues?" is a puzzle about rows and columns. Each letter in each square gets a row and a column; no
two letters of one square may share a cell. If that has a solution, the free cells can be filled and real
squares exist. If not, the cipher is impossible on that grid with any squares.

## What was run (`attacks/139_free_square_capacity.py`, 1.4 minutes on 8 cores)

An exact backtracking solver (`kryptos/idea139_freesquares.py`): union-find merges equal labels, then values
are handed out with symmetry breaking and cell / row / column checks. Every pairing w = 1..48, every block
start, both output orders, four grids, four-square and two-square: 37,632 settings per text, on K4 and 100
shuffled K4s. 0 undecided for K4 (14 in all controls together). Every "feasible" answer was rebuilt into four
real squares and checked by decrypting the clue letters.

13x2 always matches 2x13 and 9x3 matches 3x9 (a turned four-square only swaps the cipher squares and the
order): 0 disagreements, a built-in check. **Planted: 57/57** random-square four-square and two-square
encryptions of English came out feasible (and 32/32 in the unit test).

## Scope limits

* Feasible means "some squares fit the 24 clue letters". The rest of the message is then unconstrained, so
  those settings are not decrypted and remain open: four-square 3x9/9x3 at w = 1 start 0, w = 21 starts 5/26,
  and hundreds of wider pairings (K4 is no different from the shuffles there).
* Fixed squares of one shape, textbook four-square rule and idea 103's two-square rule, both output orders;
  the pair rule i, i + w in blocks of 2w; no transposition, no nulls in the text, clue positions exact.

## Surprising

The two-square result is an exact confirmation of idea 103's quick necessary test: the same 4 settings below
w = 29 survive (9x3 at w = 16 and 27). For four-square, K4's zero at w = 7 is stronger than the controls (95+
of 100 feasible) but it comes from Bean's known repeated cipher pair at w = 7, not from anything new.
