# Idea 102: the encoder loses the place in the key inside a clue word

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No setting passes on K4 with 7 or more checks. K4's near misses are within the range the
shuffled controls give, and none of them decrypts to anything like English.

## The idea

Sanborn dropped a letter carving K2 and misspelled his own K1 key. In a hand Vigenere the commonest slip is losing the
place in the key: one key letter skipped or used twice, after which every later letter is one place off. Idea 68
covered a slip between the clues and idea 81 one wrong clue letter. A slip *inside* EASTNORTHEAST or BERLINCLOCK
breaks every periodic test on that block and had not been tried.

## What was run

`attacks/102_lost_place_in_key.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea102_slip.py`.

* Slip before any letter of a block (12 places in clue 1, 10 in clue 2), key letter skipped (+1) or used twice (-1).
* Key rules: repeating key of length L; sliding key (differences repeat with length L) in two readings: the slip is in
  the key word (`slide-word`) or in the running key (`slide-stream`: a skipped place gives a sum of two word letters,
  a doubled place gives a zero difference).
* Test A: one block alone with one slip, every L that leaves 7+ checks (408 settings per key stream).
* Test B: one slip in either block, the other block unslipped at any key offset (any slips or a restart between the
  clues), L = 2..12 (10,076 settings).
* Test C: one slip in each block, any offset, L = 2..12 (109,440 settings; the plan asked for 2..8, 12 was cheap).
* Tableaux: A-Z, KRYPTOS and 666,991 dictionary keyword alphabets (plain and both transposition-mixed forms), five
  layouts, Vigenere and Beaufort: 6,669,928 key streams, 799,884,445,472 exact settings.
* Passing settings are rebuilt and decrypted (key offset before clue 1 and after clue 2 free by -2..2, best cut
  between the clues) and scored on the big model.
* The fast search looks at a setting only when its first forced equality holds; a test checks it returns exactly what
  the plain loop over all settings returns. Pre-filter: PASS.

English scores above -5.1 on the big model; the planted texts read back at -4.3 to -4.7.

Planted answers, 4/4 found with the slip point named:

* repeating 7-letter key, key letter skipped before letter 27: test B, 17 checks, 97/97 letters read back;
* sliding 5-letter key, key letter used twice before letter 68 (inside BERLINCLOCK): test B, 17 checks, 97/97;
* running key of length 6 with a slip in each block and one more between them: test C, 16 checks, 97/97;
* repeating 4-letter key with two slips in clue 1 and one in clue 2: test A on clue 2 alone, 7 checks (the rest of
  the text cannot be read back in this case, as expected).

## Surprising

K4's near-miss count (199) is 2.4 times the chance figure and above five of the six full-size shuffled controls, but
one control gives 211. The counts are clumpy: related alphabets give the same key stream, so one accident is counted
many times (K4's largest clump is 34, shuffle 1's is 84). Counted as distinct layout+setting, K4 has 88 against 36-56
on the controls, and 6 six-check misses against 0-2 (chance 2.9). That is a mild excess with no 7-check pass behind it
and decryptions that are noise (-7.34, word coverage 0.26), so it is not called a signal.

## Scope limits

* One slip of exactly one place per block. Two slips in one block, or a slip of two places, are not covered.
* Only repeating and sliding keys. Progressive, autokey, stacked and chain keys with a slip were not run.
* Test A needs 7+ checks, so a block alone only decides short keys (repeating L up to 6 on clue 1 and 4 on clue 2; sliding up to 5 and 3).
  Longer keys need both blocks (tests B and C, up to L = 12). Keys longer than 12 with a slip cannot be decided.
* Keyword alphabets from the dictionary only; no hidden alphabets, no transposition layer.
* A slip in the plaintext (a dropped clue letter) contradicts the published clue positions and is not tested.
