# Idea 107: one word per worksheet line

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No test passes on K4; nothing to decrypt.

## The idea

The K4 plaintext survives as strips cut from a handwritten sheet, and the K1 worksheet is written in short
clusters. Suppose the sheet had one word per line. Then a letter's key depends on its place inside the word
(its column), and each line may bring a letter of its own, a row word down the side. Idea 66 tested a key
that restarts at each word with nothing added; idea 65 tested row word + column word on grids of fixed
width. Ragged lines were never tested.

## What was run

`attacks/107_one_word_per_line.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea107_lines.py`, tests in `tests/test_idea_107.py`.

Four key rules (mod 26, in the tableau's own numbering):

* **add**: key = a[line] + b[column] (column key plus a free letter per line)
* **speed**: key = a[line] x column + b0 (one start letter, each word its own speed; column from 0 or 1)
* **own speed**: key = a[line] x column + s[line]
* **slide**: the strip is moved on by d[column] and never put back, no line letter. (With a line letter the
  sliding form is the add rule again, so it needs no separate test.)

Column counted from the word start or the word end. Lines all Vigenere, all Beaufort, or taking turns.
Word breaks in the clues: EAST|NORTH|EAST, EAST|NORTHEAST or EASTNORTHEAST, with BERLIN|CLOCK or
BERLINCLOCK. That makes 262 tests, each with 9 to 21 exact checks (the headline case, EAST|NORTH|EAST +
BERLIN|CLOCK with the add rule, has 14). A test passes only if every check holds. The check lists are
verified in the tests against the mod-13 / mod-2 solver for all 262 tests.

Key streams: A-Z, KRYPTOS, every dictionary keyword alphabet and both transposition-mixed forms
(666,993 alphabets), five layouts: 3,334,964 key streams, 873,760,568 tests. The run takes 3 minutes.

## Numbers

| | passes | most checks a failed test held | tests that held exactly 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| K4, all alphabets | **0** of 873,760,568 (chance 0.00001) | 6 | 401 |
| 8 shuffled K4s, all alphabets | 0 each | 5 or 6 | 9, 43, 87, 31, 34, 19, 118, 62 |
| 3 shuffled K4s, a third each | 0 each | 5, 5, 6 | 9, 7, 44 |

Planted: **6/6** among 33,350 decoy alphabets, each found by the exact test and read back by a search over
word breaks (49 to 63 of the 73 letters outside the clues right, 0 to 6 wrong; big model -4.36 to -5.09).
The planted texts are K1-K3 sentences with their real word breaks: add with column key UNDERGROUND and row
word LANGLEY, column from the word end, a speed per word, lines taking turns, slide, own speed and start.
The true setting was not the only survivor: 1 to 5 neighbouring tests that the same key also satisfies
passed too (a coarser parsing, the more general own-speed rule, once the sister alphabet NULLIFIES). No
decoy tableau of a different word passed, and the true setting always read best.

## A result that needs no dictionary

EAST is enciphered twice: FLRV (letters 22-25) and GKSS (31-34). If both EASTs stand in the same columns of
their lines and the lines have the same form, the line letter moves all four cipher letters by one amount in
the cipher alphabet. R -> S and V -> S would then put R and V on one place. So 68 of the 262 tests cannot
pass for **any** pair of alphabets, hidden or not. That includes the idea's headline case: EAST, NORTH, EAST
as three lines with one column key and a line letter, in Vigenere, Beaufort, or taking turns, counted from
either end of the word. It also kills EAST|NORTHEAST when the column is counted from the word end.

## Surprising

K4 has more 5-check near misses than any of the 8 full shuffles (401 against 9-118). The cause is one
letter pattern, not a key: the key under NORTH (cipher QQPRN) steps evenly on 1,364 key streams, against
312-627 for four shuffles and about 380 by chance, because plain N and O are neighbours and sit under the
doubled Q. Each such stream trips up to 32 tests that share those five checks. It goes no further: streams
where two clue words both step evenly number 10 for K4 against 4, 12, 25, 4 for shuffles, and no test holds
more than 6 checks on K4 or on any control.

## Scope limits

* Lines are single words. Lines that are sentences, phrases or a fixed number of words are not covered
  (only the two whole clue phrases as lines were also tried).
* Keyword tableaux from the repository vocabulary only, except the 68 clash-dead tests, which fall for any
  alphabets.
* The row word is left free, so the result does not depend on it spelling anything. Not tested: a line
  letter that multiplies the column key, a column key that depends on word length, a clue position off by one.
* The word-break reader was exercised on planted texts only, since K4 and the shuffles left nothing to read.
  It leaves words under three known letters as `?`.
