# Idea 106: K4 letters as word counts through the solved K1-K3 text

**Verdict: dead in scope.** K4's best is 10 of 24 clue letters, 20 shuffled K4s give 9 to 11, and 24 are needed.

## The idea

Scheidt compared K4 to ciphers given to agents and pilots in case of capture: simple, not mathematical, kept in
the head. The letter codes taught to air crews hid a message in an ordinary text, found by counting words.
Sanborn: the first three answers hold clues to the fourth, "just be able to read". Idea 85 counted letters and
found nothing; its worker suggested counting words. So: each K4 letter is a number, the number picks a word of
K1-K3, and one letter of that word is the plaintext.

## What was run

`attacks/106_word_counts_through_k1_k3.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea106_words.py`); the whole run took about
a minute and a half.

* **Rule W (walk):** move on d words from the last word used.
* **Rule B (window):** word number = start + a x i + d.
* **Rule C (pairs):** one K4 letter gives the word, its neighbour the letter number inside the word.
* **Texts (56 word lists):** K1, K2, K3 plaintexts and the Morse text, alone and joined in order; spelling as
  carved or corrected; X (and K3's last Q) as words or dropped; K2 ending ID BY ROWS or X LAYER TWO. The word
  lists are checked letter for letter against `data/sculpture`.
* **Letter taken:** first, second, third, fourth or last of the word.
* **Counts:** place in A-Z or KRYPTOS order, forwards or backwards, extra advance a = -30..30 (this includes
  numbering from 0 or 1 and reading the text backwards), every start word, ring and line.
* Every W or B setting fixes all 97 letters, so the test is exact.

* Mean per setting: 1.43 of 24 (W and B), 0.95 (C). With no extra advance (the plain rule) K4's best is 8.
* K4's best setting: rule B, K1-K3 as carved with X dropped and LAYER TWO, third letters, KRYPTOS numbering
  counted backwards, advance -22, start 53. It reads `MREEMWCOEUE?EVAAEADRSEESENOSSEESSTSSATONEXSEAY...`:
  gibberish, -6.55 per quadgram on the big model (English needs -5.1, noise is about -6.5), word cover 0.13.
  The most English-looking of the 24 readings with 9+ clue letters scores -6.49.
* Rule C read strictly (letter number at most the word's length) can reach only 5-13 of the 24 clue letters on
  any list, so it cannot be right in that form. In its plain form it halves the message, so no clue position
  applies; 876,800 halved readings were searched for the clue words: BERLIN 0, CLOCK 0, NORTH 0, EAST 817
  (shuffles: BERLIN 4 in one shuffle, NORTH 0-108, EAST 648-1303).
* Planted: walk on first letters (plain A=1..Z=26, ring), walk on last letters, a moving window, a window on a
  line, a walk on a line, and overlapping pairs. All found at 24/24 at the planted setting and read back.

## The surprise: word counting cannot carry English either

The idea's premise was that word starts are rich enough. On K1-K3 they are not.

* **0 of 200** ordinary English messages of 97 letters could be encoded by the walk or the window, for any of the
  five letter positions and any advance 0..30. A walk dies after a median of 6 letters on first letters (3 on last
  letters); the longest got 41. Even `THISISATEST` cannot be walked through the first letters.
* Reason: the 163 first letters are T31 S13 A11 I11 W10 ... with only 7 E, 2 N, 4 R and no J, V, Z. The last
  letters are E32 S24 D19 T17 Y16 N14 with 2 A, 2 I, 3 O and no B, J, K, L, P, V, Z. One fixed letter position
  never holds both the vowels and the consonants a message needs within 26 words.
* Overlapping pairs with a wrapping letter number: 1 of 200.
* So the planted messages had to be strings of English words chosen so that each next letter was reachable.
  That limit is the method's, not the search's.

## Pre-filter

The window rule gets PASS, but only with a crutch: 33% of English letters had no word in their window and were
written as themselves, so the card is not reliable. The walk has state and would be FLAGGED for K5 as in idea 85.

## Scope limits

Dead only for these word lists and this word division, one fixed letter position (1-4 or last), counts of at
most 26 words plus a fixed advance of -30..30. Not covered: a count that also depends on a key word, a rule that
changes the letter position from word to word, a different word division, nulls, a transposition before or
after, texts not on the sculpture.

## Follow-ups

1. Read K1-K3 itself as the carrier: first/last letters of its words taken at a fixed interval, no K4 involved.
2. Let one K4 letter give both numbers (word count = value mod m, letter number = value div m, m = 2..13).
