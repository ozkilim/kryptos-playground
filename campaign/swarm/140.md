# Idea 140: the key steps with each repeat of the cipher letter

**Verdict: dead in scope.** It is impossible by proof in the two natural settings. It finds nothing anywhere
else, where the shuffled K4s also find nothing. All 4 planted keys were found. And the method can't encipher
English at all.

## The idea

Idea 109 gave each plain letter its own counter into a key word. Here the counter sits on the cipher letter:

    C_i = Tab(P_i, W[n_i mod L])      n_i = how many times C_i occurred before

A reader can work out n_i from the ciphertext alone, so decryption is easy, letter by letter.

## What was run

`attacks/140_cipher_letter_counters.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea140_cipher.py`), about 2 minutes in the slot.

* **Proofs, for every tableau (hidden ones too).** A fixed key letter maps plain to cipher one-to-one. So clue
  positions with the same residue n mod L need "same plain" exactly when "same cipher".
  * Counting from K4's first letter: positions 22 (E->F) and 31 (E->G) both have n = 1. Dead for every L and
    every word.
  * Counting from the start of K1's ciphertext: dead for every L = 1..26.
  * Over all 769 start points in K1K2K3, 487 are dead for every L.
  * 3,478 (start, L) settings are left with 7+ checks (2,030 distinct groupings).
* **Exact test on keyword tableaux.** 666,993 alphabets x 5 layouts x Vigenere/Beaufort = 6.67M key streams.
  Variant Beaufort has the same equalities as Vigenere and is handled at the join. Survivors are joined with
  264k dictionary words and K1-K3 phrase windows (any rotation, key letter read in three alphabets), and are
  also tried with W free (missing key letters filled by a hill climb).

**Planted: 4/4.** FUTWA (KRYPTOS, count from K4's start), LAWNED (mixed dictionary tableau, from K1's start),
PRECONFIGURE and UNRIGHTEOUS (from points inside K1-K3). Each was named with its key, alphabet and start and read
back exactly.

## Surprise: the method can't encipher English

To encipher, the writer needs a cipher letter c whose own next key letter turns P into c. There may be no such
letter, or several. A doubled plain letter at the start already blocks it, unless W[0] = W[1]. I took 1,000
English messages with random dictionary keys and searched with full backtracking (more than a hand writer
could do):

* 941 can't get past 10 letters;
* 1 gets past 20;
* none gets past 30.

So the planted texts had to be made backwards, from random ciphertext. They are not English. Idea 109's model
was ambiguous to decrypt. This one mirrors it: easy to decrypt, almost impossible to encrypt.

## Scope limits

* One key word, counters starting at 0, counting from K4's start or from a point of K1K2K3's carved ciphertext.
* Known keyword tableaux only away from the two proofs. Hidden alphabets were not tested at the other starts.
* Not covered: a separate start count per letter, counts through other texts, two key words.
