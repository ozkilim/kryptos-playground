# Idea 82: two paper rotors in series with hidden wirings

**The idea.** Idea 72 closed one rotor with a hidden wiring. Two rotors in series is the smallest machine that test
cannot see: `Y = R1[P+s] - s`, then `C = R2[Y+t] - t`, with a fast turn `s` (a speed, a key word, a short key) and a
slow turn `t` that stays put inside each clue block.

**The test.** The plan said each block is "one single-rotor system". It is not quite: a block is a rotor followed by
a fixed hidden substitution, so idea 72's wire test does not apply as it stands. The right version: every clue letter
ties one contact of R1 to one contact of R2 (`R1[P+s] - R2back[C+t] = s - t`). The 24 clue letters draw a graph between
the two rotors. A wrong setting dies when a closed loop does not add up or when two contacts of one rotor land on the
same letter. A survivor is scored by its number of closed loops. Putting block 2 at `t = d` (d = 0-25) is exactly the
question "are the two blocks' wirings a shift of each other". No wiring is guessed.

Two forms were run (`attacks/82_two_paper_rotors.py`, helper `kryptos/idea82_tworotor.py`):

* **H**: two different hidden wirings (the idea as written).
* **B**: one strip used there and back (the second rotor is the first read backwards, at its own position). Half the
  unknowns, so it can really be decided. Survivors are completed: the pieces of wiring must fit on one strip, then all
  97 letters are decrypted and scored as English.

Fast turn: 26 speeds, previous cipher letter, running sum of the cipher, 248,495 words (plain, sliding, +1 per letter;
A-Z or KRYPTOS numbering), every key of length 1-5 (H) or 1-4 (B). Slow turn: block 2 shifted 0-25, one step per pass
of the key, and for the small families one step inside a block, a step every 2-12 letters, or a second speed.

**Numbers** (598,807,128 settings on K4, 23 minutes).

| | K4 | shuffled K4 seeds 1, 2, 3 |
|---|---|---|
| form H, settings with 5+ loops (the threshold) | 0 of 229.8M | 0, 0, 0 |
| form H, most loops of any survivor | 3 (11 settings) | 3, 3, 3 |
| form B, pass the loop test with 2+ loops | 20 of 369.0M | 6, 10, 0 |
| form B, of those: pieces fit on one strip | 0 | 0, 0, 0 |

Each shuffled K4 got the small families and shorter keys in full and a different third of the words and longest keys.

Planted messages: **6/6**. Form B: word key, 3-letter key and constant speed all found, and the message read back
(67-85 of 97 letters, English score -4.2 to -4.8; the threshold is -5.1). Form H: the same three found, but the planted
message had to be redrawn until its true setting had 5 loops (11, 6 and 2,395 draws).

**The honest count of forced coincidences.** This is the main finding. With two different hidden wirings there are 52
unknown contacts and 24 clue letters, so loops are rare. Share of true settings that reach 5 loops (3,000 random draws
each): constant speed **0.0%**, word key **1.5%**, 3-letter key **14.3%**. In form B: 98.6%, 99.6%, 99.6% reach the
2 loops needed.

**Verdict: dead in scope for form B; form H gives no signal and mostly cannot be judged.** K4 looks exactly like the
shuffled K4s in both forms. Nothing was found.

**Scope limits.**

* Form H is **not** ruled out. At least 86% of true settings (short keys) and 98-100% (words, speeds) would pass
  unseen. No key length is properly decidable; 3-letter keys, at 14%, were the best case measured.
* Form B, words: slow strip start 0-25 only when block 2 is not shifted, start 0 only when it is. Words and keys of
  length 4 only with both rotors turning the same way. Keys over 4 letters only if they are dictionary words.
* Not covered: one strip used twice in the same direction (R2 = R1; needs a backtracking search), slow steps inside a
  clue block for word keys, three rotors, a reflector.

**Surprises.**

* The loop test alone is not enough in form B: 20 K4 settings pass it with up to 3 loops, and shuffled K4s do the
  same. All die when the wiring pieces are put on one strip. A first run without that step would have listed 4
  "hits".
* On a planted short-key message thousands of other keys of the same length pass too (15,800 in H, 5,172 in B, 66 of
  which fit a strip). The test proves the key length and block structure, not the exact key. In form B the best
  reading is still the planted text.
* The clue pre-filter passes the model (PASS, score 1.0) and marks the lag-4 run hint as explained (13 times over
  chance). That is a reason to keep form H on the open list, not evidence for it.
