# Idea 141: king moves on any hidden grid

**Verdict: no signal.** A grid exists almost everywhere, so the clues do not constrain a free grid.

## The idea

Write the 26 letters in a grid. The key is a string of compass moves; each plain letter steps one cell (or stays)
to give its cipher letter. Idea 123 tried 14.5M grids built from listed alphabets and none made all 24 clue pairs a
king move (best: 2 pairs broken). A hand-made grid is on no list. So: is there ANY arrangement of the letters that
works, and how few different moves does it need?

## What was run

`attacks/141_king_moves_any_grid.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea141_anygrid.py`, test in `tests/test_idea_141.py`.

* Only the clue letters matter. K4's clue pairs make a graph of 21 letters and 21 different lines (R→P comes twice;
  S→S and K→K are stays). The letters must go on different cells so that every line joins king-move neighbours.
  The other 5 letters fill what is left.
* Exact backtracking (always the letter with the fewest cells left), with a node limit that reports "undecided".
* 66 grid shapes: width 3–13, 26 or 27 cells (one empty cell anywhere), no wrap / columns wrap / both wrap.
* Fewest different moves D (stay counts, as in idea 123), searched upward from an exact lower bound.
* K4 and 200 shuffled K4s: 13,266 shape × text cases, 28 minutes. Every placement found was re-checked by a
  separate function: 0 failures.

## Numbers

| | K4 | 200 shuffled K4s |
|---|---|---|
| Shapes where a grid exists (of 66) | 58, none undecided | min 17, median 55, max 66; 32.5% have ≥ 58 |
| Smallest D on any shape | 4 | 3: 9, 4: 85, 5: 88, 6: 18; 47% are ≤ 4 |
| Shapes with D ≤ 4 | 35 | mean 11.6, max 66; 15.5% have ≥ 35 |
| Smallest D minus the lower bound | 0 | 0: 65, 1: 113, 2: 21, 3: 1 |

* **Impossible for any arrangement (K4):** width 12 without wrap and width 13 in all conventions, 26 and 27 cells
  (8 shapes). These are also the hardest for shuffles (impossible for 132–177 of 200).
* **Not unique.** At the smallest D there are 8 to more than 20,000 placements of the clue letters per shape, and
  more than 20,000 on every feasible shape when D is not limited.
* **Planted: 11/11.** English moved by random king moves on random hidden grids (8 with any move, 3 with only 3–4
  allowed moves). All feasible, the true grid passed the check, D found was never above the true grid's, and with
  7 letters freed the full list of solutions (46–2,220 grids) contained the true grid.

## The one thing that looked interesting

K4 reaches its lower bound (3 moves plus stay) on 35 shapes; the average shuffle does so on 11.6. The cause is plain:
K4's clue graph has only **one closed ring**, because one pair repeats and two letters stay. A graph that is nearly a
tree can be laid out with any proper choice of moves. The 10 shuffles that also have one ring average 36.0 shapes at
their lower bound; with 2 rings 19.7, with 3 rings 4.2. So K4 is ordinary for its ring count, and the ring count
itself (1 ring: 10 of 200 shuffles) is the known repeat-and-stay feature of the clues, not a grid effect.

## Scope limits

* Feasible is not a decryption. Grid and key are both free, so no text was produced or scored.
* One grid for plain and cipher letters; moves of one cell or a stay; the empty cell cannot be stepped on.
* Not covered: a separate cipher grid, longer moves, other layouts, widths 2 or 14+.
* Grid counts are clue-letter placements, capped at 20,000, not reduced by symmetry.
* 43 shuffles have 1–9 undecided shapes (node limit), mostly narrow or wide grids with wraps; some of their D
  values are upper ends. K4's values are all exact.
