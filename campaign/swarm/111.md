# Idea 111: vowels and consonants take their key letters from two separate words (or the key holds)

**Verdict: dead in scope. No signal.**

## The idea

Sanborn spoke of two separate systems, Scheidt of masking English and of "the E against the Z". A hand way
to do both: two key words, one used up by the vowels and one by the consonants, each moving on only when it is
used. The key place of a letter then depends on the text before it, so every position-based test of rounds 9-11
is blind to it. Three models were tested, all on the 24 clue letters, all exact:

* **A, two words.** Vowel word of 1-8 letters, consonant word of 1-16. Inside a clue the counters are known
  (EASTNORTHEAST has 5 vowels and 8 consonants, BERLINCLOCK 3 and 8); both phases at BERLINCLOCK are free.
* **B, one key that holds or skips.** Key of 2-16 that does not move, or moves two, at or after a letter of a
  set: vowels, consonants, the letters of KRYPTOS, each single clue letter (plain or cipher side), a cipher
  letter equal to the one before. 117 distinct patterns. On A-Z, KRYPTOS and 84 theme alphabets also all 8,191
  subsets of the 13 clue plaintext letters, key of 2-12.
* **C.** Class read from the cipher letter (with or without Y); vowels in Vigenere and consonants in Beaufort
  or the reverse, in both models.

Tableaux: 666,993 alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS, dictionary keyword alphabets and their two transposition-mixed
forms), five layouts. A hit needs 7 checks (coincidences minus log26 of the phases tried).
Pre-filter: FLAG on the K5 clue only (the key place depends on the text), as for every text-dependent key.

## Numbers

| | settings with 7+ checks | chance expects |
|---|---|---|
| K4, two words (A/C), 81.6 billion settings | **0** | 0.008 |
| K4, holding key (B), 210.7 billion settings | **0** | 0.044 |
| K4, all subsets on theme alphabets, 2.16 billion | **0** | 1.5e-9 |
| 3 shuffled K4s, a third of the alphabets each | 0, 0, 0 in every model | |

Near misses of the two-word model (4 to under 7 checks): K4 0 on all alphabets; shuffled 3, 0, 0 on a third
each. The uniform-key estimate (16 per ciphertext) is too high for real key streams, because a clue letter that
repeats with different cipher letters can never share a key letter; a side check on 600,000 uniformly random
streams gave 3 near misses against 0.7 expected, so the test itself is not missing them. K4 sits with the
controls. Total 294,499,206,348 settings, 16.5 minutes.

Planted answers, 4/4, each read back 97 of 97 letters and each with the most checks of all hits:

1. vowel word of 3, consonant word of 6, alphabet LEU, 14.1 checks. 529 other key streams also passed: they are
   alphabets equal to LEU on the clue letters (LEVEL, LEW, LEX ...), plus weaker echoes.
2. key of 7 that holds after a vowel, PICKSHAFT~cols, Beaufort, 16.4 checks.
3. vowel word of 2 in Vigenere, consonant word of 5 in Beaufort, TURBAN~keyed, 16.3 checks.
4. key of 6 that moves two onto K, N or R, PYRAMID (subset search), 17.5 checks, the only hit.

## Scope limits

* Two words: 7 checks are needed, so roughly p + q above 15 is undecided; a vowel word of 8 or more is
  unconstrained and then only a consonant word up to 8 can be seen.
* Class by cipher letter is weak on K4: the clue ciphertext has no A, E, I, O, U, so it reduces to a plain
  repeating key of 1-16 with a free phase at BERLINCLOCK.
* Holding key: named sets only on the full dictionary; arbitrary sets only among the clue plaintext letters, on
  86 alphabets, key up to 12. Holds of exactly 0 and skips of exactly 2. No restarts, no three classes.
* No hidden alphabets, no transposition layer. Controls on a third of the alphabets each.

## Surprising

The two-word cipher cannot be read back uniquely. The reader has to guess whether each letter was a vowel, and
both guesses often decrypt consistently. With the correct key and both clues fixed, 30 planted English messages
had between 384 and 41 million consistent readings (median about 19,000); the right one has to be picked by a
language model. That is a reason of its own to doubt this family as a designed cipher, apart from the clue test.
