# Idea 134: one hidden strip used twice in the same direction

**Verdict: dead in scope.**

**The idea.** Idea 82 closed the case of one strip used there and back, and showed that two different hidden strips can't be judged. A hand encoder with one strip would more likely send the letter through it twice the same way: C = R[R[P+s]-s+t]-t. The wiring R can be anything. Each clue letter says "R sends x = P+s to some a, and R sends a + (t-s) to C+t". That gives 24 equations against about 19 letters of freedom in a wiring, so a random setting has about a 4 in 100 million chance of fitting.

**What was run.** `attacks/134_one_strip_twice.py` with helper `kryptos/idea134_onestrip.py`. For each setting, an exact backtracking search over R:
- Clues that share an exit letter tie their entry contacts into pieces.
- Each piece has one unknown, and its allowed values are worked out up front.
- The pieces are then placed one by one, with forced contacts propagated.
- A test that does not depend on the slow offset's start drops most settings before any search.

The fast solver was checked against a plain backtracker on 10,400 settings: same answers, same solution sets.

Offset rules searched:
- constant speeds of both offsets
- a fast rule (speed, previous cipher letter, running cipher sum) with a slow rule (constant per clue block with a jump, a step every 2–26 letters, a constant speed)
- every key of 1–5 letters, plus a random 26,052 of the 6-letter keys
- 21,510 theme and common words in three forms

Both orders were run, with A-Z or KRYPTOS faces. That makes 3,678,511,824 settings, and no search hit its cap.

**K4.**
- Constant speeds, fast/slow pairs and keys of 1–5 letters: 0 survivors.
- Words: 4 settings fit, but they are only 2 readings, because each appears at two slow starts 13 apart:
  - ALNUM, word + 1 per letter: −7.11 per quadgram on the big model.
  - INVOKED, sliding: −7.75.
- English needs −5.1. Both are gibberish around the clue words.

**Controls.** I ran 3 shuffled K4s, about 1.3 billion settings each. Each got a third of the words and of the 5-letter keys, and a different sample of 6-letter keys.
- Survivors: 2, 0 and 4.
- Best readings: −7.55, none, −6.94.

K4's result looks the same as the controls.

**Planted answers: 3/3.** Each was found and read back with every forced letter right:
- a 3-letter key with a per-pass step: 72 of 97 letters
- a pair of constant speeds: 72 of 97
- a 6-letter key with a block jump: 89 of 97

English scores were −4.1 to −4.4.

**Surprising.**
- Keys of 1–5, 7 and 9 letters, with the other offset fixed inside each clue block, are impossible for any strip. The same plain letter at the same key place would have to give the same cipher letter, and K4's clue letters break that. This holds for any per-block fixed substitution, not just this model.
- Survivors come in pairs 13 slow-starts apart, which points to a symmetry of the model.

**Scope limits.**
- 6-letter keys were sampled (0.22%). Keys longer than 6 were not run.
- Constant-speed slow offsets were run only with keys of 1–3 letters.
- The 236,000-word big dictionary was not run.
- Not covered: three or more passes, two different strips, mixed directions.
- The machine was overloaded by other workers, so the run took about 2.5 hours instead of 40 minutes.
