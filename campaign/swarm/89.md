# Idea 89: alphabets that are not keyword alphabets

**Verdict: dead in scope.** None of 140,752 hand-listable alphabets that are not keyword alphabets fits K4 in the
exact tests, and the one weaker test shows no signal. The shuffled K4s look the same or better. All 5 exact planted
answers were found; the sentence-key score found 3 of 4.

## The idea

Every exact test since round 9 took its mixed alphabets from A-Z, KRYPTOS or a dictionary word. A hand cipher can mix
an alphabet in other simple ways, and these can be listed:

* **Text orders.** Read a text and write each letter down the first time it appears (a keyphrase alphabet). The texts
  are the sculpture's own, because "the answers to the first three passages contain clues to the fourth": the K1, K2
  and K3 plaintexts as carved and corrected (ILLUSION, UNDERGROUND, LAYER TWO, DESPERATELY), the three joined, the
  K1-K4 ciphertext, and the Morse text. Every starting letter, forwards and backwards, stopped after 1 to 26 different
  letters (so every phrase starting anywhere is included), with the unused letters appended in A-Z or Z-A order.
* **Fixed orders.** 7 typewriter orders (QWERTY rows and columns, QWERTZ, AZERTY), 6 Morse-code orders (by code
  length, by dot/dash sort, by duration), 14 frequency orders (ETAOIN tables and the letter counts of the sculpture's
  texts).

After dropping duplicates and the 1,720 that are already dictionary keyword alphabets, **140,752** are left: 27 fixed,
9,047 text orders read to the end, 122,574 phrase orders, 9,104 reversed forms. The clue pre-filter gives PASS.

## What was run

`attacks/89_non_keyword_alphabets.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea89_orders.py`), 17 minutes.

1. **Battery** (`kryptos.round11.battery`): the key values the clues force, on five layouts (M/M, M/A-Z, A-Z/M,
   M/KRYPTOS, KRYPTOS/M), Vigenere and Beaufort; repeating, sliding, progressive, autokey, two stacked keys, chain
   addition; 7 or more exact checks. 1,407,520 key streams.
2. **Sentence key** (attack 57): the 13 + 11 forced key letters scored as English, 560 views per alphabet (78.8M).
3. **Join** (attack 63's hash join): a repeating key of length 1-20 with two alphabets, a new one against each of
   666,994 dictionary alphabets (plain, transposition-mixed, KRYPTOS) both ways round, and new against new. That is
   207.6 billion alphabet pairs per key length and form, 8.3 trillion settings. Every fit is decrypted where the clues
   fix the key and scored on the big English model. The join was checked against brute force on a 600 x 600 sample
   of alphabets with 16 clue letters (same fits).

## Scope limits

* Only the listed orders. An arbitrary written chart stays open. Unused letters are appended in A-Z or Z-A order only.
* Battery: one new alphabet with itself, A-Z or KRYPTOS, and only the battery's rules and ranges, 7+ checks.
* Join: repeating key only, lengths 1-20, partner from the dictionary set or the new list. At lengths 16-20 fits
  happen by chance and the result rests on reading them. Lengths 21+ were not run.
* The sentence-key score is weak. It sees a key only if its 24 forced letters score above about -4.7, and it missed
  one of four planted keys. English keys only. Treat that part as "no signal", not "dead".
* Reversed forms were added only for the fixed and read-to-the-end orders. For the battery and the join a reversed
  alphabet is the same test with Vigenere and Beaufort swapped, so nothing is lost there.
* Overlap: idea 86 had already closed repeating and sliding keys for ONE unknown alphabet of any kind at most
  lengths. What is new here is the other key rules, the sentence key and the two-alphabet join.

## Anything surprising

* K4 has far fewer join fits than the three controls (1 against 47-89). It is not a signal: 12 more shuffles (run
  separately, join only) gave 1 to 72 fits, and 3 of the 15 shuffles have 4 or fewer. The fits cluster at key length
  18 and come from the A-Z tails that these alphabets share; whether a text has any depends on three or four letter
  pairs. In a 600 x 600 sample of alphabets with only 16 clue letters kept, K4 still has none at length 18 where a
  shuffle has 123.
* The three controls' best readings are all -6.76. They are different readings; the clue words inside each one
  dominate the score.
* The list is bigger than the "few thousand" the plan expected, because every stopping point of every phrase was
  included (122,574 phrase orders). The plan's own list is the 27 fixed and 9,047 read-to-the-end orders.
