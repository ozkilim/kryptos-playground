# Idea 90: a worksheet row keys the next row

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No sign that K4 is an autokey looking one worksheet row back.

## The idea

The K3 worksheet is 42 letters wide and the two K4 clues start exactly 42 letters apart. With the `?`, K4 is a
7 x 14 grid. If each row is keyed by the row above, the key letter is the letter 7 or 42 places earlier (plaintext
or ciphertext), and EASTNORTHEAST would be the key for BERLINCLOCK. Earlier rounds tested lags 1-6 on keyword
tableaux and gave lags 30-52 only a quick look on A-Z / KRYPTOS.

## What was run

`attacks/90_row_keys_next_row.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea90_rowkey.py`). The model is
`key[i] = word[i mod p] + sign * number(letter at i - L)`, Vigenere or Beaufort form. The test is exact: over all clue
pairs, key minus fed letter must be one constant (p = 1) or one constant per class mod p (p = 2, 3, 4).
A setting passes with 7 or more checks (pairs minus word length).

* **Plain feed:** lags 7 (10 pairs) and 8 (8 pairs); lags 34-48 with the second clue moved by -3..3, wherever 8+
  pairs remain (lag 42 in place: 11 pairs).
* **Cipher feed** (only the keyed letter has to be a clue letter, so far more can be tested): lags 1-66 with the clues
  in place (24 pairs up to lag 21, 11 pairs for lags 34-63), and lags 34-48 with the second clue moved by -3..3.
* **Tableaux:** A-Z, KRYPTOS and every dictionary keyword alphabet in plain, `~cols` and `~keyed` form (666,993), five
  layouts, fed letter numbered in M, A-Z or KRYPTOS, added or subtracted.

In all 221 (feed, lag, shift) tests and **31.6 billion settings**, 13 minutes.

| planted answers | 5/5 found | 7 to 23 | yes: -3.3 to -4.8 |

K4's one pass is `WORTHLESSNESS~cols`, KR/M, Beaufort, cipher feed 61 back, word of 4: 11 pairs, 7 checks, the lowest
that counts. It forces the 36 letters after position 61 and they are noise:
`EYBERLINCLOCKWQTIMQQZBFFNETMJZFSLJCY`. About 7 billion of the settings sit at exactly 7 checks and each passes by
chance once in 26^7 (8 billion), so roughly one such pass per full run is expected. The three shuffled thirds
(one full pass together) happened to give none. **Nothing passes at lag 42, 7 or 8**, the lags the idea was about.

Planted: plain feed 42 back under a 42-letter primer (10 checks); cipher feed 7 back (23); plain feed 41 back with the
second clue moved +2 and a word of 2 (8); cipher feed 50 back with a word of 3 (8); plain feed 7 back with a word of
3 (7). All found on their own tableau among 9,000 sampled alphabets, and the forced letters matched the planted text.

## Pre-filter

Lags 7 and 8 get FLAG: the K5 clue (same words, same places, same cipher letters) fails, because another message
gives another key. Lag 42 gets PASS, but only because the probe's second message shares *both* clue words; if K5
shares only BERLINCLOCK it would fail too. The written reason to test anyway is the 42 coincidence.

## Scope limits

* Plain feed at lags 9-12 (5 or fewer checks) and 13-33 (no clue pairs at all) cannot be decided from the clues.
* Dictionary keyword alphabets only; no hidden alphabet, no transposition, no mixed plain/cipher feed.
* Words on top only of length 2-4 and only where 7+ checks remain (none at plain lag 8).
* Clue shifts -3..3 only for lags 34-48. Controls saw a third of the alphabets each.

## Surprises

Nothing odd. One point worth keeping: with a cipher feed every lag up to 66 is testable, because the fed letter is
always known. That closes cipher-feed autokey at lags 21-66 on keyword tableaux, which idea 74 (lags 1-20, M/M only)
had left open.
