# Idea 122: the sentence strips were enciphered one at a time

**Verdict: dead-in-scope.** K4 gives 0 passes in 37.0 billion exact tests (chance predicts 0.28). The 3 shuffled K4s give 0, 1 and 0.

## The idea

Sanborn cut the plaintext into sentence strips and handled them out of order. Suppose each sentence was enciphered on its own, or the sentences were enciphered in a different order from the one carved. Then the key runs on unbroken inside each clue, but its place at BERLINCLOCK has nothing to do with the 42 letters between the clues. Every joint test so far assumed the carved gap. Idea 68 allowed the gap to be off by 1-3 letters, and idea 43 allowed a restarting repeating key on 18 alphabets. Nothing more was tested.

## What was run

`attacks/122_strips_enciphered_alone.py`, with helpers in `kryptos/idea122_strips.py`. The 13 and 11 forced key letters are treated as two separate runs, and the second run gets a free phase. The carved phase is left out because it was tested before. A second split, EAST | NORTHEAST | BERLINCLOCK, makes three runs with two free phases. The search covers 666,993 keyword tableaux in five layouts, Vigenere and Beaufort: 6.67M key streams with 5,550 settings each. The rules are:

* repeating and sliding keys, L 2-16;
* plain and cipher autokey, lags 1-6, with a restarting word of L 2-16 on top;
* progressive keys, p 1-12, with a free pass count;
* two stacked words p<q<=9, each with its own free phase (exact loop test).

Over three runs only repeating, sliding and autokey+word were tested, with L 2-9. Every test is exact and a pass needs 7 or more checks. Autokey over three runs needs 8, because at 7 its 2,456 settings per stream would let in 0.26 chance passes. The full run took 11 minutes.

## Numbers

* **K4:** 0 of 37,018,100,400 tests pass, against 0.28 expected by chance. There is nothing to read.
* **Controls:** 3 shuffled K4s, each on A-Z, KRYPTOS and a different third of the alphabets (12.3 billion tests each). They give 0, 1 and 0 passes against 0.09 expected each. The single pass reads as gibberish: big model -8.23, where English needs better than -5.1.
* **Gauge (5-6 checks):** K4 has 3 passes against 9.2 predicted. The controls have 2, 1 and 1 against 3.1 each. K4 and the controls fall short of the uniform-chance figure by the same factor, so K4 is not unusual. The prediction treats the key values as random and independent, which they are not exactly.
* **Planted:** 4/4, each hidden among 20,000 decoy alphabets. Each was found with its phase named and read back 97/97:
  * repeating key of 9 restarted at a sentence start between the clues (phase 7);
  * sliding key of 6 with the two sentences enciphered in swapped order (phase 5);
  * stacked 4+7 with both words restarted (phases 3 and 5);
  * repeating key of 7 restarted at NORTHEAST and again between the clues (phases 3 and 6).

  Big-model scores were -4.31 to -4.58. The reading stage tries every break point from 34 to 63 and picked the true sentence start, or one letter off where the letters there agree. All passes in the planted runs were on the planted alphabet, with no decoy passes. For the repeating-9 message, the same key also satisfies 41 other settings on that alphabet.

## Scope limits

* Keyword tableaux only: no hidden alphabets.
* Not decided: L above 16, and stacked pairs with q above 9.
* Three runs cover only repeating, sliding and autokey+word, with L up to 9.
* Autokey feeds are numbered on the keyword alphabet only.
* Not covered: more restarts inside the clue blocks, and a restart inside BERLINCLOCK (closed in round 8).
