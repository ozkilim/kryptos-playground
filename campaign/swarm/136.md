# Idea 136: plain autokey lags 5-12 on a half-hidden tableau

**Verdict: dead in scope.** Where the test is exact, nothing passes. Where it has to read, K4 reads no better than
shuffled K4s.

## The idea

Idea 117 left plaintext autokey at lags 5-12 back and 7-12 ahead alive on every Latin square. Ideas 90 and 100 tested
it only with both sides of the tableau known. This run tests the half-hidden case: one side is one of 666,993
dictionary alphabets, the other side is free. The model is `pos_c(C) = pos_p(P) + s*num(F) + c`, where F is the plain
letter d back or ahead and s = +-1 covers Vigenere, Beaufort and the variant. The fed letter is numbered on the plain
alphabet, on A-Z or on KRYPTOS.

## What was run

`attacks/136_half_hidden_autokey.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea136_half.py`, test in `tests/test_idea_136.py`. The
run took 24 minutes. Every pair of clue letters in one class mod d gives one equation. When the fed letter is
numbered on the plain alphabet and the cipher alphabet is the known one, the equations stay linear right across the
29-letter gap, so they give 4-6 real checks. All other cells keep only 0-3 checks. Each cell got a chance estimate
(from its check ranks times the share of systems with distinct places) and a tier:

| tier | cells (K4) | K4 passes | shuffled K4s (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, exact, under 0.5 chance passes | 9 | **0** (0.3 expected) | 0, 0, 0 |
| B, exact plus reading on the big model | 35 | 4,243 (est. 3,073) | 2,041 / 2,548 / 1,206 |
| C, counted only | 58 | 1.21 M (est. 0.83 M) | 1.53-2.35 M |
| D, no check at all, not run | 66 | - | - |

The pass counts are ordinary: the controls run 0.5-2.8 times their own estimates, and K4 runs 1.4 times. Keyword
alphabets are not random permutations, so they pass a little more often than the bound says.

**Reading.** All 4,243 K4 passes were read, by beam search with the clue letters as constraints and then annealing.
K4's best is -5.56 per quadgram with 5 clue letters missed:
`ITLERONGCEREMPFUIEMNLEASTNORTHEASTKRYATIFIRDAGEANNARAUPTRALTACTBERLINCLOCKINPACKDOIHTOMEIFULJFSLT`. English needs
about -5.1, and planted texts read at about -4.5. The controls' best readings are -5.48, -5.74 and -5.47. That is
noise.

**Planted: 3/3.** Each was found among 9,000 alphabets and was the best reading:
* plain-known lag 7 back: 89/97 letters;
* cipher-known lag 9 ahead: 94/97;
* cipher-known lag 5 back with A-Z numbering: 97/97.

Read back with the true alphabet given (5 each):
* cipher-known lag 11 back: 4/5;
* plain-known lag 9 ahead: 2/5;
* plain-known with A-Z numbering, lag 7 ahead: 3/5;
* plain-known with A-Z numbering, lag 7 back: 2/5.

**Pre-filter:** FLAG on the K5 clue, as for every key that follows the message. It was built anyway because K5's
shared words may not share cipher letters, and the clue checks are exact.

## Scope limits

* Only 9 cells are decided exactly: cipher-known with plain numbering, lags 5-7 back, 7 ahead, and 9 ahead with the
  minus sign.
* The plain-known side with A-Z/KRYPTOS numbering is judged mostly by counts, because the reader recovers only about
  half of the planted texts there.
* 66 cells have no check and were not run. These are most of lags 8-12 ahead on the plain-known side, lag 12 back,
  and A-Z/KRYPTOS numbering at lags 8 and up on the cipher-known side.
* Only dictionary keyword alphabets on the known side. No key multiplier, clue blocks in place, plain feed only.

## Surprises

* The plan expected 8-14 forced places at every lag. In fact the plain-known side only gets checks where cipher
  letters repeat, so it is weak at every lag.
* The strong half is the mirror, cipher-known with plain numbering: there the unknown plain places enter twice, and
  the chains stay linear across the gap.
