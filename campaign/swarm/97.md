# Idea 97: a key written with few symbols

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No hit, K4 looks like the shuffled K4s, planted keys flagged 4/4.

## The idea

Scheidt said he "could have moved to a different math base"; Sanborn says to "delve into that particular clock".
If the key is a list of numbers (digits in base 2-16, clock hours 1-12, Roman numerals), then however irregular
the list is, the key uses few different values. On the right tableau the 24 key values forced by the clue letters
would sit in a small window of consecutive values (digits 0-9 are a window of 10) or on 7 or fewer values (the
Roman letters I V X L C D M). This needs no rule for the key at all. The community had shown that no alphabet makes
all 24 values digits 0-9, but only for a window starting at 0 and one way of reading the key.

## What was run

`attacks/97_few_symbol_key.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea97_fewsymbols.py`.

* 666,993 alphabets: A-Z, KRYPTOS, every dictionary keyword alphabet and its two transposition-mixed forms.
* Five layouts each (M/M, M/A-Z, A-Z/M, M/KRYPTOS, KRYPTOS/M), four views (C-P, C+P, P-C, -(C+P)):
  13,339,856 settings. A view and its negative give mirror-image values, so the window numbers were computed once
  (6,669,928 key streams); tests that name values (digits 0-9, a base, Roman letters) were done for both signs.
* Per key stream: smallest circular window holding all 24 values; number of distinct values; most clue letters in a
  window of 5, 8, 10, 12, 16; most on any 2, 3, 4, 7 values; Roman letters with the key letter read in A-Z, KRYPTOS
  or the tableau alphabet. The same for each clue block alone.
* 50 shuffled K4s on the same alphabets (the plan asked for 5), plus 300 more for one block-alone number.

K4's window of 12 is reached by 17 of the 50 shuffles and beaten by 5; its 8 distinct values by 31. The three K4
streams with a window of 12 (BAGGIT~cols and CAGIT~cols on M/A-Z Beaufort, values 8-19; ISOTRIMORPHOUS~keyed on
M/M Vigenere) and the rest of the top 20 were read by eye: no number pattern, no repeats worth a second look.

Idea 60 found that the best 2/3/4 fixed rows on A-Z and KRYPTOS cover 9/12/14 clue letters. Over every keyword
tableau the best are 13/17/18, and the shuffles give 12-14/15-17/17-19: still chance.

The earlier community entry says no alphabet keeps all 24 values below 16. With five layouts and both signs, 13
streams keep them below 14. That is also chance (15 of 50 shuffles do better).

**Planted, 4/4 flagged with the exact key** (random, non-periodic keys, searched among all 666,993 alphabets):
digits 0-9 (PREVIOUS, window 10), base-12 digits (WHOSE, window 12), clock hours 1-12 (SHA~cols, window 11),
Roman letters (NAMES~cols, 6 values, 24 of 24 Roman by name). Two ranked first outright; the other two were
tied or passed by two different keys (a neighbouring alphabet, or one chance stream at window 11).

## Two things worth knowing

1. **A window of 12 is not rare enough by itself.** The plan called a window of 12 or less a hit. Over 6.7 million
   key streams it turns up by chance in a third of shuffled texts, and K4 has it. What separates a real key is the
   neighbourhood: keyword alphabets are close to each other, so the planted digit key shows up on 38 streams at 12
   or less (11 at 10 or less) across alphabets like PREMIOUS and IMPERVIOUS. K4 has 3 streams at 12 and none
   below. So digits and bases up to 10, and Roman numerals, are cleanly absent; bases 12-16 and clock hours are
   ruled out by the weaker argument (no cluster, no pattern in the values).
2. **One number stood out and then did not.** EASTNORTHEAST alone fits a window of 5 on OYSTERWIFE~keyed (A-Z/M,
   Beaufort): `7 4 3 6 6 7 7 4 6 5 6 6 7`. None of the 50 shuffles reached 5. A side check
   (`attacks/97_side_block1.py`, not a logged experiment) on 300 more shuffles found 5 in 6 of them (2%). It was
   the only one of 24 compared numbers where K4 led all 50 controls, and on the same tableau the BERLINCLOCK
   values are spread over the whole alphabet (`0 1 7 24 25 4 12 18 17 18 16`), which a few-symbol key would not
   do. Chance.

## Scope limits

* Nothing about keys that use most of the alphabet.
* Only dictionary keyword alphabets (plain and transposition-mixed), A-Z and KRYPTOS; one alphabet against itself,
  A-Z or KRYPTOS. A hidden alphabet is not covered (see the follow-up: it can be decided exactly).
* Additive tableaux only, clue positions as published, no transposition.
* Bases 12-16: see point 1. The single-block numbers are too weak to decide anything alone.
