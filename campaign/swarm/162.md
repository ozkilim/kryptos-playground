# Idea 162: shifting matrices taken literally (the coordinate slip)

**Verdict: dead-in-scope.** No grid of the tested shapes can give the clue letters, and the cipher leaves
English rougher than K4.

## The idea

Put the alphabet in a hidden grid. Write each plain letter as two digits, row above column. Slide the lower
line of digits s places along and read each column of two digits back as a letter. Every cipher letter then
has one coordinate from its own plain letter and one from a letter s places away.

## What was run

`attacks/162_coordinate_slip.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea162_slip.py`, test `tests/test_idea_162.py`.

* Grids: 2x13, 13x2, 3x9, 9x3, 4x7, 7x4, 5x6, 6x5. The 26 letters may sit anywhere; spare cells are empty.
* **Part A, the idea as stated:** one line at home, the other slid by s = -45..45; row or column at home;
  digits straight or crossed (cipher row taken from a plain column); every added constant on the straight
  lines. 41,400 settings.
* **Part B, an extra:** both lines slid (u, w = -45..45, u ≠ w), which is the same cipher with the text
  displaced. 128,160 settings.
* Each setting is decided exactly for *any* grid: the clue letters say "X is in the row of Y", and a
  backtracking search looks for a placing with no two letters in one cell. Two end rules: open (only
  statements with both letters inside the 97) and wrapped (the digit lines wrap round).
* Survivors: every dictionary keyword grid (220,318 alphabets, by rows or columns, spare cells first or last).

The 308 open-end survivors on K4 are all on 4x7 / 7x4 / 5x6 / 6x5 with both lines slid 34 or more places:
at least 36 of the 97 letters fall off the end and one clue word is not tested at all. None has a keyword
grid, so there is nothing to decrypt or read.

**Planted: 8/8.** English messages on keyword grids (5x6 slip 3; 3x9 slip 21; 6x5 by columns, slip -7;
4x7 slip 5 with a constant; 2x13 slip -2 with a constant; 5x6 both lines slid; crossed 5x6 and 6x5) all pass
the any-grid test, the planted grid is found among the 220,318 alphabets, and the text reads back 97/97.
Before the run, 194/194 random-grid encryptions were judged feasible.

## Why the stated idea dies (it can be checked by hand)

With the row line at home, each cipher letter is in the same row as its own plain letter. The 24 clue pairs
chain together: E–F, F–O, O–Q, Q–N, N–B, N–T, T–V … In all, **21 letters land in one row**. The longest line
on any of these grids is 13. A constant added to the home line does not help: K stays K at letter 74 and S
stays S at letter 33, which forces the constant to 0. The idea's own hand check found the clash only for
s = ±1; it holds for every s. Shuffled K4s chain 9 to 23 letters (median 19), so K4 is ordinary here.

## Two side findings

* **The cipher is too rough.** On 1,000 English 97-letter texts per shape the index of coincidence averages
  0.048 – 0.054 (5th percentile 0.039 – 0.044). K4 is 0.0361. Only 0.0 – 0.8 % of messages come that low.
* **Spare cells get hit.** A row digit from one letter and a column digit from another often point at an empty
  cell. On random grids an English message avoids every empty cell in 100 % (2x13), about 20 % (3x9), 4 %
  (4x7) and 0.5 % (5x6) of cases. K4 has 97 letters and no other mark.
* Crossed digits only work when the digit exists on the other axis. In practice that is a 5x5 square with one
  letter set aside (5x6 filled by columns with Z alone in the last column); that form was planted and found.

## Scope limits

One grid for writing and reading; 26 letters; the eight shapes above; one fixed slip for the whole message,
up to 45 places. Constants only in Part A on straight lines. Not tested: a slip that restarts in blocks
(periodic bifid proper, where a cipher letter can take both digits from rows), two different grids, digit
lines permuted instead of slid, any transposition before or after. Part B's open-end survivors are ruled out
only for dictionary keyword grids, not for a free grid. Clue positions are taken as exact.
