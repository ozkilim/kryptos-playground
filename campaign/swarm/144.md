# Idea 144: a key word on one tableau, a second tableau turned by a short list of steps

**Verdict: dead in scope.** Nothing passes with 8 or more checks, the 7-check survivors are what chance leaves, none
of them spells a dictionary key word, none reads as English, and all 4 planted answers were found.

## The idea

Idea 98 closed a key word on alphabet A stacked with a second tableau B that turns by one fixed step per letter. A
strip moved by hand can just as well move 3, then 11, then 3, then 11 ("shifting matrices"). So here the turn is a
repeating list of k steps:

    key layer    X -> A[ +-A^-1(X) + a_(i mod p) ]
    turn layer   X -> B[ +-B^-1(X) + T(i) ]      T(0) = t,  T(i+1) = T(i) + s[i mod k]

in both orders, Vigenere or Beaufort per layer. The clue pre-filter gives PASS. Not covered before: idea 98 is the
case where all steps are equal, and those lists are left out of the counts here.

## What was run

`attacks/144_step_pattern_second_tableau.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea144_steps.py`). For given B, step list and t
the turn layer is known, so each clue letter shows the key layer's key value; a key word of length p needs equal
values wherever i mod p is equal. Checks = 24 minus the key slots the clues touch. Exact, no key guessed. The scan
keeps one bit per (step list, t) and ANDs big integers; it agrees with a plain one-at-a-time scan on all 140,608
settings of a test pair.

* **k = 2:** 650 step lists x 26 starts x 2 orders x 4 types = 135,200 settings per pair, on idea 98's 888,238 pairs
  (7,310 ordered pairs of A-Z, KRYPTOS and 84 theme alphabets; 220,232 dictionary alphabets against A-Z and KRYPTOS
  in both roles).
* **k = 3:** 17,550 step lists, 3,650,400 settings per pair, on the 7,310 theme pairs only.
* **Key lengths 1-23** (7+ checks; 17, 18 and 23 have exactly 7). Length 24 (5 checks) is a chance gauge only.
* Survivors with 7+ checks: the forced key is matched against 248,397 words and the text is decrypted and scored.
* **Controls:** 3 shuffled K4s, each on all theme pairs and a different third of the dictionary alphabets.

The full run took 11 minutes.

## Scope limits

* Both alphabets must be on the lists. k = 3 was run on the theme pairs only, not on dictionary alphabets.
* Step lists of length 2 and 3, counted over the whole text from letter 1. No restart at carved rows or rows of 21.
* Key length 1-23. At 17, 18 and 23 the test has only 7 checks, so it leaves chance survivors; they are ruled out
  by the word join and the reading, not by the check count. Length 24 and up is not decided.
* The word list is 248,397 words (data/web2.txt plus theme and common words), not the 264k of the plan.
* Same alphabet in both layers skipped (it folds into one key). No hidden or mixed alphabets, no transposition
  between layers, no third layer.
* The controls saw a third of the dictionary alphabets each, so their counts are compared after scaling.

## Anything surprising

* K4's k = 2 count at exactly 7 checks is a little high: 60 against about 45 from chance, or 20.3 per third against
  15, 11, 8 in the controls. It is not a signal: nothing reaches 8 checks (where a real key word of length up to 16
  would land), 4 of the 60 are one family (A-Z turned, key on TALLOWROOT, keys sharing most letters), no key is a
  word, and K4's best reading (-6.50) is level with the best control (-6.49). The spread between shuffled texts is
  just as wide at k = 3 (6, 8, 20 against 10 predicted).
* Cross-check: among the constant-step lists the scan found again idea 98's one whole-text survivor (KRYPTOS then
  FASTIDIOUSNESS, step 22 from 17, length 23, 7 checks).
