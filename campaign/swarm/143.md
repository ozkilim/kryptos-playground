# Idea 143: two-step autokey chains on one hidden alphabet, lags 19-22

**Verdict: no signal.** The test is now exact for every cell. It kills 18 of K4's 32 cells and cannot kill the
other 14. Shuffled K4s look the same.

## The idea

One hidden alphabet numbers the plaintext letter, the cipher letter and the fed letter:
`X[C] = a X[P] + b X[K] + E` with a, b = +-1 and K the plaintext letter d places back or ahead. At lags 19-22
no two clue letters are one lag apart, but 9-11 pairs are two lags apart, and the unknown letter between them
drops out. Each pair gives one equation in the 26 unknown places. Idea 100's worker saw that the constant E
drops out when ab = 1. It also goes when ab = -1: move the alphabet along by E places. So each cell asks
one question: can 26 letters take 26 different places mod 26 and satisfy 9-11 equations with no constant?

Idea 117 asked this and left 14 of 32 cells undecided, because it lists solutions and there were too many.

## What was run

`attacks/143_two_step_chains_hidden_alphabet.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea143_chains.py`, test in
`tests/test_idea_143.py`. 72 seconds.

* 4 lags (19, 20, 21, 22 with 9, 11, 11, 9 chains) x look-back/look-ahead x 4 sign forms = 32 cells.
* "Impossible" is a proof: two letters forced into the same place mod 13 and mod 2.
* "Possible" is a witness alphabet found by backtracking, then checked by running the cipher itself through
  every chain.
* The same on 50 shuffled K4s, and on 160 planted texts.

* K4's 18 impossible cells are exactly idea 117's 18. All 14 of its undecided cells are possible.
* Possible cells per lag: 19: 4, 20: 2, 21: 3, 22: 5.
* Dead for K4: lag 20 look-back in all four forms. `C = P + K` and `C = -P - K` are dead at every lag and
  direction except lag 22 look-ahead `C = P + K`. Eleven controls have as few or fewer of these.
* 38 controls have more possible cells than K4, 4 the same, 8 fewer. K4 is ordinary.

**Planted: 160/160.** Five English texts with the clue words in place for each lag, direction and form, on
random hidden alphabets with a random constant. Each reads back, the true alphabet satisfies the equations,
and the true cell is decided possible. The other 31 cells of a planted text are impossible 8-22 times (median
15), so a wrong cell dies about half the time.

**Pre-filter:** FLAG on the K5 clue, as for every key that follows the message. Built anyway: the test is
exact, takes a minute, and closes the 14 cells idea 117 left undecided.

## Scope limits

* One alphabet on all three letters, coefficients +-1, fed letter exactly d back or ahead, clue blocks in
  place.
* "Possible" only means the 24 clue letters cannot rule the cell out. Half of all wrong cells pass too, so
  the test cannot pick a lag.
* No alphabet was searched for and no reading of K4 was made. A possible cell leaves 15-17 free dimensions.
* Extra table, lags 15-18 and 23-26 (1-7 chains): K4 has 4-8 of 8 cells possible per lag, inside the control
  range each time.

## Worth knowing

* Every "impossible" for K4 (18) and for the 50 controls (781) is a plain forced collision; none needed the
  exhaustive search. No cell was left undecided in K4, the controls or the planted texts. So for these
  systems idea 117's collision test was already complete; its "undecided" cells were simply possible.
* The constant E can always be set to 0 when one hidden alphabet numbers all three letters.
* A full alphabet at these lags would fix 65-75 of the 97 letters (41-51 outside the clues). Reading the 14
  surviving cells needs a search over alphabets inside the chain solution space. That is a different, much
  harder job, listed as a follow-up.
