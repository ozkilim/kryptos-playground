# Idea 94: each word is the key for the next word (word autokey)

**Verdict: dead in scope.** 0 of 128.4 billion settings pass. K4 never beats the shuffled controls.

## The idea

Scheidt wanted something simple that can be remembered. A word autokey needs no key text: after a primer word, every
plaintext word is enciphered with the word before it. Inside the clues the word before is known (EAST keys NORTH, NORTH
keys EAST, BERLIN keys CLOCK), so 14 letters have a known key letter and only one free constant: 13 exact checks.
Earlier fixed-lag autokey tests could not see this, because the lag changes with every word length.

## What was run

`attacks/94_word_autokey.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea94_wordkey.py`, test in `tests/test_idea_94.py`. 470 seconds.

* **Word splits:** EAST|NORTH|EAST, EAST|NORTHEAST, EASTNORTH|EAST, EASTNORTHEAST; BERLIN|CLOCK, BERLINCLOCK.
* **Key word:** the previous word or the word two back; forwards or reversed; its plaintext or its cipher letters; a
  longer word wraps round the key word, or runs on into its own letters. For cipher feed and run-on the unknown word in
  front of each clue matters, so lengths 1-12 were all tried. 5,348 different tests in all.
* **Tableaux:** A-Z, KRYPTOS and every dictionary keyword alphabet in plain and two transposition-mixed forms (666,993),
  five layouts, Vigenere and Beaufort form, fed letter numbered on the plain or cipher side, added or subtracted.
  A setting passes if all pairs give the same constant with 7 or more checks.
* **Any tableau:** the same pairs checked against every tableau with distinct rows and columns.
* **One hidden alphabet** on all three sides, four forms (C = P + K, P - K, K - P, -P - K): 21,392 linear systems mod 26;
  a solution with the letters in different places is required.

## Numbers

| | passes (7+ checks) | most pairs agreeing of 14 (plain feed) | cipher feed, of 24 (1,000 alphabets) |
|---|---|---|---|
| K4, all 666,993 alphabets | **0** | 8 (21 settings; 1,121 at 7) | 11 |
| 5 shuffled K4s, a fifth of the alphabets each | 0 | 9 (1 setting; 50 at 8; 1,363 at 7) | 11, 11, 11, 11, 11 |

Chance alone predicts a best of about 9 of 14 over this many settings, so K4's 8 is ordinary.

**Any tableau.** EAST|NORTH|EAST keyed forwards by the previous plaintext word is impossible on every tableau, hidden or
not: the T of NORTH is keyed by the T of EAST, and the T of the second EAST by the T of NORTH. Same plain letter, same
key letter, but K4 shows R at letter 28 and S at letter 33. The other seven full plain-feed readings have no such clash.

**Hidden alphabet.**

| | all 24 clue letters (cipher feed), 12,624 systems | 32 full plain-feed systems | all systems with 14+ pairs that have solutions | undecided (14+ pairs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K4 | all impossible | 31 impossible, 1 has solutions | 17 | 218 |
| shuffled K4s | all impossible | 3, 1, 0, 0, 0 have solutions | 77, 37, 19, 31, 47 | 476, 267, 367, 186, 255 |

K4's one surviving full plain-feed system is EAST|NORTH|EAST, key word reversed, run-on, C = K - P. Its alphabet
stays too loose to decrypt anything outside the clues; two of the five shuffled K4s have survivors of the same kind.

**Planted: 7/7.** Five messages with the real clue words, on random dictionary tableaux hidden among 9,000 others:
found with 13, 16, 23, 13 and 15 checks, and letters 34-96 read back 61-63 of 63 (English -4.39 to -4.49). Two on random
hidden alphabets: with cipher feed (24 equations) the true alphabet came back; with plain feed (14 equations) the true
setting was kept but the alphabet cannot be pinned down.

**Pre-filter:** FLAG on the K5 clue (a shared word encrypts the same in 2% of message pairs), as expected for a key that
follows the message. Built anyway because it costs minutes and gives 13 exact checks. It "explains" the lag-4 run hint
(as odd as K4 in 7.3% of probes against 0.33% by chance), which is what made it worth the run.

## Scope limits

* Only the listed word splits, with the clues exactly in place. Another split is untested.
* Key word = previous word or word two back. Word two back with plain feed has only 4 known pairs: not decidable.
* Key = constant plus or minus the fed letter. No multiplier and no repeating word on top.
* Hidden alphabet: one alphabet on all sides only. 17 K4 systems have solutions and 218 are undecided (6 or more free
  dimensions). They cannot be read, so they are not excluded. Shuffled K4s have as many or more.
* Controls saw a fifth of the alphabets each. The cipher-feed agreement count used the first 1,000 alphabets only
  (the exact pass test covered all of them).
* No transposition before or after.

## Worth knowing

* The T-under-T clash is a one-line argument that needs no alphabet at all. The same clash test could be run on every
  earlier model that feeds known clue letters as key.
* K4 passed nothing, so there was no K4 text to read. The English step was exercised only on the planted messages.
