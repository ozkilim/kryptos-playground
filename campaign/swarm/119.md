# Idea 119: the key added like a number, with carries

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No key setting passed, and none of the shuffled controls passed either.

**The idea.** Scheidt said he "could have moved to a different math base". Every exact test so far adds key and
text one letter at a time, with no carry. Suppose instead the message and a repeated key word were added as two
long numbers, in base 26 (A=0) or base 27 (A=1). Then each cipher letter is plain + key + a carry of 0 or 1. The key
values the clues force would be off by one in an irregular pattern, and every repeating-key test would miss.

**Why the test is exact.** Inside a clue block we know both the plain and the cipher letters. Fix the one carry
that enters the block. Then the key digit at the first letter worked is forced, so its sum and the next carry are
known, and the rest of the block follows. Each block gives two key vectors, so the two blocks together give at
most four 24-value vectors per stream. The usual exact key-rule tests then apply directly, with honest check
counts. No approximation is involved (`kryptos/idea119_carry.py`).

**What was run** (`attacks/119_key_added_with_carries.py`):
- **Alphabets:** 666,993 keyword alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS, theme words, common words and the big dictionary, each
  plain and in two transposition-mixed forms), in 5 layouts.
- **Settings:** three forms (C=P+K with carries; C=P-K and C=K-P with borrows), carries running right to left and
  left to right, and bases 26 and 27.
- **Key rules:** repeating, sliding and progressive keys, L 1-12, needing 7 or more checks.
- **Row restarts** (carved rows 4+31+31+31, or rows of 7, 14, 21, 31, 42; key running on or restarting): run on
  the 58,390 A-Z/KRYPTOS/theme/common-word alphabets only.
- **Rest of the battery** (autokey, two stacked keys, chain addition): run on the 256 A-Z/KRYPTOS/theme alphabets
  only.
- **Run time:** 14 minutes.

- **Planted answers:** 12/12 recovered. Key words had 5, 8 and 11 letters. They were planted on the KRYPTOS tableau
  (including row-restart models) and on dictionary tableaux, in both carry directions and all three forms. Each
  read back 97/97, and no other survivor gave a different reading.
- **Equivalent forms:** A-Z on KR/KR is the KRYPTOS tableau, and subtracting a key equals adding its complement. So
  one planted answer also passes in other forms, but those decrypt to the same text.

**Scope limits.**
- Keys longer than 12 letters were not tested.
- No other bases (for example decimal letter numbers 01-26), and base 26 numbered from 1 was not tested.
- Two key words added at the same time with carries of 0-2 were not tested, nor two separate carry passes.
- No hidden alphabets, and no transposition layer.
- The big dictionary ran with one long number only (no row restarts).

**Worth noting.** Since a carry depends only on the letters next to it, a carry model is a tiny change to a plain
Vigenère, and the exact reduction is cheap. The prefilter gave a FLAG: a word shared with K5 at the same place
encrypts the same in only 74% of probe pairs, because a carry can come in from the next letter. This is a soft
conflict with the K5 statement, not a fact the model breaks.
