# Idea 92: short key formulas with the tableau alphabet hidden

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No short formula key fits K4 under any hidden alphabet, in the six layouts tested.

## The idea

Idea 54 tested every short formula for the key (position, number sequences, grid row and column), but only with A-Z
or KRYPTOS as the tableau alphabet. Sanborn's chart need not be either. Here the alphabet X is any order of the 26
letters. The key is still known once a formula is picked, and that is enough: each clue letter says "the cipher letter
sits k places after the plain letter in X". K4's 24 clue letters tie 21 letters into one piece and close 4 loops, so 4
sums of key values must come out exactly (in the Vigenere form: the key is 0 where S encrypts to S and where K
encrypts to K, plus two longer loops). Then the 21 letters must all land on different positions.

## What was run

`attacks/92_formula_keys_hidden_alphabet.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea92_hidden.py`, test in `tests/test_idea_92.py`).

* **Key:** `k = b1*f1 + b2*f2 + b3*f3 + c mod 26`, b = 1..25, any constant, up to **three** ingredients (the idea asked
  for two; the test is linear, so a hash join made three cheap). 134 ingredients, 128 different on the clue positions:
  i, i^2, i^3, triangular numbers, primes, Fibonacci, 2^i, 3^i, digits of pi and e, column / row / row*column for
  widths 2-40, the carved 4/31/31/31 layout. No neighbour-letter terms (idea 55 has those).
* **Layouts:** one hidden alphabet on both sides, Vigenere form `X[C] = X[P] + k` and Beaufort form `X[C] = k - X[P]`;
  hidden plain side against A-Z or KRYPTOS; hidden cipher side against A-Z or KRYPTOS.
* **Test:** exact. The union-find of `freealpha.py` worked out once per ciphertext as integer vectors, checked against
  the plain solvers in the test file. Survivors are laid out on one alphabet, the free letters are tried in every
  order, and the best reading is scored on the big English model.
* 170.9 billion (formula, constant) settings per ciphertext; the whole run took 8 minutes.

With two ingredients only, K4 gives 3,074 loop passes of 132 million (Vigenere form) and 2,810 of 10 million
(Beaufort form), 0 fits.

The 30 chance fits on the shuffles are 1, 6, 1, 9, 11 and 2 key streams. All but one sit on shuffles whose clue
letters fall into 2-3 pieces, which is a weaker test than K4's single piece. The other 14 shuffles give 0, like K4.
On 2 shuffles with 2 or fewer loop tests the three-ingredient search was left out, because almost everything fits there.

Planted: random alphabet with a progressive key `3*i`, a quadratic key `5*i^2 + 10*i` and a 7-wide grid key
`7*row + 4*col`, each in both forms, two half-hidden cases and two three-ingredient keys. The texts were redrawn until
the clue letters gave 4 or more loop tests in one piece, as K4's do (1-17 draws). Six more on the first text drawn
(4-7 loop tests, 1-2 pieces) were also read back in full.

## Measured against predicted

A uniform guess (key values as random numbers) says 303,771 settings pass K4's loop tests per form; 701,652 and
698,652 did. The shuffles show the same 2-7x excess. The reason is that grid keys are not random: many are flat on the
clue positions (two values only: 69,072 and 65,160 of K4's passes), and many formulas are the same key written
differently. The same guess says about 2 final fits per form for K4; none appeared. On the shuffles that have fits the
guess was 4-10x too high, so 0 on K4 is what the controls lead one to expect, not a deficit worth reading into.

## Scope limits

* Up to three ingredients from this library. Not four or more, not neighbour-letter terms, not the `?` as a cell.
* One hidden alphabet used on both sides, or hidden on one side with A-Z or KRYPTOS on the other. **Two different
  hidden alphabets cannot be tested this way** (about 1 loop; nearly every key fits). A hidden alphabet against a
  dictionary keyword alphabet was not run.
* A formula key with a second step on top (transposition, a mask) is outside this test.
* With three ingredients the one-alphabet layouts are at their noise limit (a few chance fits expected); they are
  decided by reading, and here there was nothing to read.

## Surprising

* A key that is always even (for example `5*i^2 + 11*i`) keeps every cipher letter on the same half of the hidden
  alphabet (odd or even places) as its plain letter. K4's clue letters tie 21 letters into one piece, and a half has
  only 13 places, so every key that is even on all clue positions is ruled out in the one-alphabet Vigenere form
  without any search.
* In the one-alphabet layouts every key comes in a family of 12 (multiply the key by 3, 5, 7, ... and stretch the
  alphabet the same way): the same reading, so "different keys" must be counted per family.
