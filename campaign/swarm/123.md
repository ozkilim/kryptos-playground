# Idea 123: compass steps on a letter grid

**Verdict: dead in scope.**

## The idea

Write the alphabet in a grid. The key is a string of compass moves: N, NE, E and so on, or stay. Each plain letter
moves one cell to give its cipher letter. If K4 was made this way, then on the right grid all 24 clue pairs are a
king's move apart. S→S and K→K are "stay".

## What was run

`attacks/123_compass_steps_on_grid.py`, with helpers in `kryptos/idea123_compass.py`.

* **Grids:** every alphabet was written in rows of width 3 to 13, with 26 cells or 27 (the ? as the last cell).
  The last row is short.
* **Wrap conventions:** three of them: no wrap, columns wrap, both wrap.
* **Alphabets:** two sets.
  * The small set: A-Z, KRYPTOS, 84 theme-word alphabets and 12 old-script orders, 98 alphabets in all.
  * 220,232 dictionary keyword alphabets.
  * That makes 14.5 million grids for K4.
* **Statistics per grid:**
  * R: the largest king distance of the 24 moves.
  * D: the number of different moves.
  * Q: how many moves lie on a compass line.
  * Each was also computed for each clue block alone.
* **Controls:** 200 shuffled K4s on the small set, and 3 shuffled K4s on a third of the dictionary each.
* **Runtime:** 60 s.

## Numbers

| | K4 | Shuffled K4s |
|---|---|---|
| Grids with R = 1 on all 24 pairs | 0 of 14.5M (chance expectation 7.7e-6) | 0 in every shuffle |
| Best R | 2 | 2 or 3 |
| Best D, small set | 12 | 10–13 (share as extreme as K4: 0.95) |
| Best D, dictionary | 9 | 8–10 |
| Grids with D ≤ 8 | 0 | one shuffle has 2 |
| Best Q, small set | 22 | 20–23 (share: 0.45) |
| Best Q, dictionary | 24 | 23–24 |

**Planted answers: 3/3 found with R = 1 at the planted grid.** The three were KRYPTOS at width 7 with no wrap,
UNDERGROUND at width 5 with no wrap, and PALIMPSEST at width 9 with 27 cells and both wraps. The reading stage read
back 54, 68 and 50 of 97 letters. A random choice among the candidates gets 35, 35 and 32.5.

The plan asked for 60 of 97, and only 1 of the 3 plants reached it. Each cipher letter allows up to 9 plain
letters, and on 12 plants an exact quadgram Viterbi plus a word split averaged about 54 of 97. So a real survivor
would have needed a human read. None appeared.

## Surprises (none is a signal)

**Block 1 alone (EASTNORTHEAST).** K4 has 620 grids where all 13 pairs are compass steps. The shuffles have none.
All 620 are width-3 grids with wrapped columns on near-A-Z keyword alphabets: BLACK, WALK, LACK and so on. In such
a grid a compass step is just a shift of −4 to +4 letters. This is Bean's known closeness of the first-block
letters, which shuffling destroys. It is not evidence for a grid. Small-shift windows were already measured by
ideas 97 and 112.

**Block 2 alone.** K4 has 2 hits on the small set (VALLEYOFTHEKINGS, width 3, both wraps), with p = 0.005. That p
is one of about 15 statistics looked at. On the dictionary it reverses: K4 0, shuffles 8, 8 and 0.

## Scope limits

* Only the listed alphabets were tried, without transposition-mixed forms. A free hidden alphabet is open; see the
  follow-up in the JSON card.
* Grids are filled row by row, and the ? is only ever the last cell.
* Moves of 2 or more cells cannot be judged.
* Wide grids with row wrap (width 9 or more) and width 13 without wrap have at most 3 rows, so every row move is
  within 1 and only columns are tested. Those grids are weak tests.
