# Idea 109: each plain letter keeps its own place in the key word

**Verdict: dead in scope.** K4 gives nothing, the shuffled K4s give the same nothing, and all 6 planted answers
were found and read back.

## The idea

Scheidt says he "masked the English language" with something simple and remembered, run with the right key
word(s). The classic hand way to flatten letter counts and still work letter for letter is a counter per plain
letter: the first E takes key letter 1, the second E key letter 2, and so on.

    C = Tab(P, W[n mod L])       n = how many times THIS plain letter has occurred before

The key letter depends on the letter's own count, not on its position. No earlier test modelled this.
The clue pre-filter gives FLAG on the K5 statement (same words in the same places would only encrypt alike if the
letter counts before them match). It was built anyway: the test is exact and takes five minutes.

## What was run

`attacks/109_letter_counters.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea109_cycling.py`), 287 seconds in the slot.

Proofs first, true for every tableau and alphabet, checked in code:

* A common numeric step per occurrence is impossible. T goes V then R, L goes V then Z: one step from V would
  have to give both R and Z.
* Counters that restart at each word are impossible. EAST is FLRV, then GKSS, with E the first E of its word both times.
* Keys of 1 or 2 letters, and any 2 tableaux taken in turn (even hidden ones), are impossible. The T's at 25 and
  34 are two occurrences apart and give V and S.
* 3 or 4 hidden tableaux picked by the counter always fit the clues (exact search), so that cannot be decided.

Inside a clue block the next occurrence of a letter is known (E 22-31, A 23-32, S 24-33, T 25-29-34, L 67-71,
C 70-73). That is 7 ordered key-letter pairs that must be neighbours in W, cyclically.

1. **Word keys.** 264,690 keys (dictionary and theme words of 3+ letters, every 4-26 letter window of K1-K3's
   plaintexts) indexed by their cyclic letter pairs. 666,993 alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS, dictionary keyword alphabets
   and their two transposition-mixed forms) x 5 layouts x Vigenere, Beaufort, variant x the alphabet the key letter is
   read in = 24,011,697 readings. Survivors are decrypted exactly from counters at 0 (every rotation of the word),
   with the clue letters forced.
2. **Keys of any letters, length 1-5.** The 7 pairs must fit a ring of 5.
3. **Counters restart at each row.** Rows of every width 2-48 with every first-row length (the carved rows are
   width 31, first row 4). A restart inside a clue block makes counts known, so groups of clue letters must share
   a key value. 168 distinct tests with 6+ checks. Any key letters.
4. **Three or four different fixed alphabets in turn.** For three: exact join (two of them must explain 16+ of the
   24 clue letters, the third the rest). For four: a bound on A-Z, KRYPTOS and 84 theme alphabets only.

Each control got every alphabet, not a third (the test is cheap). Row tests: chance predicts 0.22 passes at 6
checks and 0.01 at 7+. Four fixed alphabets from the 86 small ones explain at most 23 of 24 clue letters: impossible.

**Planted, 6/6:** COMPASS on a dictionary tableau (Vigenere); PALIMPSEST on a transposition-mixed dictionary
tableau (Beaufort); ABSCISSA on KRYPTOS (variant) on natural text with its own clue letters; a 4-letter non-word
key; SHADOW with counters restarting at the carved rows; three different fixed alphabets in turn. Texts came back
at 91-96 of 97 letters, English -4.02 to -4.74 (bar -5.1). No survivor read anything else.

## Scope limits

* Tableaux must be known. Hidden alphabets are not covered, and 3-4 hidden tableaux cannot be decided at all.
* Keys must be in the word and phrase list, or be any letters up to length 5. A longer key that is not in the list,
  or a separate key per letter, cannot be decided.
* Counters start at 0 at K4's first letter (every rotation of the word tried). Counters carried over from K1-K3, or
  a different start per letter, are not covered.
* Row restarts are tested only where a restart falls inside a clue block (6+ checks).
* Four fixed alphabets at dictionary scale: not decided.

## Surprises

* **The cipher is ambiguous.** Two letters whose next key letters lead to the same cipher letter can swap, so even
  with the right key a planted text has thousands of valid readings and comes back with a few letters swapped. The
  receiver would face the same thing. That is a reason to doubt the idea beyond this search.
* The "chance" figure for word keys (11.4 chain fits, assuming independent uniform key values) is not met by K4 or
  any control (all 0). The formula is right for uniform keys (checked: 346 found against 255 predicted on random
  values), but keyword alphabets are near copies of each other, so real counts are clumpy (pair hits 2 to 31). The
  shuffled K4s are the fair comparison.
