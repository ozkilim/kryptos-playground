# Idea 98: a key word on one tableau, a steadily turning second tableau

**Verdict: dead in scope.** K4 gives nothing above chance, it sits inside the shuffled K4s on every count, and all
4 planted answers came back as the single survivor.

## The idea

K2 ends LAYER TWO, Scheidt taught "shifting matrices" and calls K4 "more than one stage". Idea 84 closed two
repeating keys on two different tableaux. Here one layer is a repeating key word on alphabet A, and the other is a
tableau B that turns by a fixed step every letter:

    key layer    X -> A[ +-A^-1(X) + a_(i mod p) ]
    turn layer   X -> B[ +-B^-1(X) + s*g(i) + t ]       s = 1..25, t = 0..25

in both orders. On one tableau the two fold into a single progressive key (closed before). On two different
tableaux they do not, and the turn hides the key word's period. The clue pre-filter gives PASS.

## What was run

`attacks/98_turning_second_tableau.py` (helpers in `kryptos/idea98_turning.py`). For given B, s, t the turn layer is
fully known, so each clue letter shows the key layer's key value outright; a key word of length p needs equal values
wherever i mod p is equal. Checks = 24 minus the key slots the clues touch. No key is guessed.

* **Alphabets.** Every ordered pair of different alphabets from A-Z, KRYPTOS and 84 theme alphabets (7,310), plus
  220,232 dictionary keyword alphabets against A-Z and KRYPTOS, in both roles (880,928). 888,238 pairs in all.
* **Per pair, 15,600 settings:** 2 layer orders x 4 types (Vigenere or Beaufort per layer) x 25 steps x 26 starts x
  3 row layouts (turn counted over the whole text; restarting at the carved rows, K4 letters 1, 5, 36, 67;
  restarting every 21 letters). Rows of 42 are the same test as rows of 21 on the clue letters (t moves by 21 s), so
  each rows-of-21 survivor is read both ways.
* **Key lengths 1-23**, all with 7 or more checks (17, 18 and 23 have exactly 7). Length 24 (5 checks) is kept only
  as a gauge that chance behaves as predicted.
* **Nothing was cut.** The plan allowed trimming the dictionary part to a few step sizes; it was not needed. The 3
  shuffled K4s got all the pairs, not a third each.

## Scope limits

* Both alphabets must be on the lists. Two dictionary alphabets at once, transposition-mixed and hidden alphabets
  are not covered.
* Key word length 1-23. At lengths 17, 18 and 23 the test has only 7 checks, so a handful of chance survivors
  appear (K4 2, shuffled 6, 7, 0); they were decrypted and none is English. Length 24 and up is not decided.
* The turn is exactly s*g(i) + t with one fixed step. A second layer that is itself a word-driven slide, a step
  that changes, or restarts at word boundaries are not covered. Step 0 is idea 84's one-letter second key.
* The same alphabet in both layers was skipped (it folds into one progressive key, closed in earlier rounds).
* No transposition between the layers, no third layer, no gap slack.

## Anything surprising

No signal, but two things worth knowing. First, the plan's "require 7+" bar is not clean here: with 15,600
settings per pair and three key lengths at exactly 7 checks, chance alone leaves about 5 survivors per text, so
the table splits "exactly 7" from "8+" and every survivor was read. Second, the test is very cheap: all 3,900 turn
settings of a pair are compared at once with `bytes.translate` and big-integer XOR, about 1 ms per alphabet pair.
The whole run (K4, three full controls, four planted searches, about 1.3 trillion tests) took about 5 minutes.
