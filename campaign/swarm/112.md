# Idea 112: a small-number key under a hidden alphabet (narrowest window any alphabet allows)

**Verdict: no signal.** The planted answers were recovered (21/21).

**The idea.** Suppose the key is a list of small numbers: digits 0-9, values 0-4 or clock hours 1-12. Then on the right
alphabet, all 24 key values forced by the clue letters sit inside a narrow window of consecutive values. Idea 97 measured
this on 667k listed keyword tableaux. Here the alphabet is completely free. For each layout an exact solver
(`kryptos/idea112_window.py`) decides whether any arrangement of the 26 letters puts all 24 key values inside a circular
window of w values. It tries w = 1, 2, 3 ... and stops at the first w that works. The solver uses backtracking with arc
consistency and an all-different matching test, so a "no" is a proof. An independent naive solver and a plain matching
check agreed on every case compared (smallest w found, and w−1 shown impossible).

**Layouts.**
- One hidden alphabet on both sides, Vigenère and Beaufort. Variant Beaufort is the mirror of Vigenère, so it gives the same answers.
- A hidden plain alphabet against A-Z or KRYPTOS.
- A hidden cipher alphabet against A-Z or KRYPTOS.
- Extra: two independent hidden alphabets.

With one hidden side, Vigenère, Beaufort and variant are the same problem, because a mirrored alphabet is still an
alphabet. Each clue block was also done alone.

**K4, all 24 clue letters.**

| Layout | K4's w | Shuffles at or below K4 |
|---|---|---|
| One alphabet both sides, Vigenère | 6 | 115/200 |
| One alphabet both sides, Beaufort | 6 | 113/200 |
| Hidden plain side vs A-Z | 12 | 24/200 |
| Hidden plain side vs KRYPTOS | 12 | 34/200 |
| Hidden cipher side vs A-Z | 12 | 39/200 |
| Hidden cipher side vs KRYPTOS | 14 | 146/200 |
| Two hidden alphabets | 3 | 149/200 |

The statistic fixed in advance was one alphabet on both sides, Vigenère: p = 0.575. The best of the 7 layouts gives
p = 0.120. Measured against each shuffle's own best layout, that comes to p = 0.325. Nothing is low, so the big-model
completion step was not run.

**Planted answers.** Three English texts × 7 layouts, with random hidden alphabets. Every time, w came out at or below the
planted window, and the true alphabet was a solution at that window. Power is the real limit here:

- **One hidden side:** all 12 plants would have been flagged. Random texts need w = 11-18, so a window of 10 or less stands out.
- **One alphabet on both sides:** random texts already fit w = 5-7. A digit key 0-9 cannot be seen at all. Keys of 0-4 or
  1-12 show only weakly (Vigenère p = 0.06, Beaufort p = 0.035).
- **Two hidden alphabets:** w = 2-5 for almost any text, so this layout has no power.

**Surprising, but not new.** EASTNORTHEAST alone, with a hidden plain alphabet against A-Z cipher, fits a window of 5.
That happens in 16 of 5,000 shuffles (0.3%; 0 of the first 200). The same block with a hidden cipher alphabet against A-Z
fits a window of 3 (3.5%). This was not fixed in advance; it is one of 21 numbers reported, so about 7% after correction.
It is the known Bean / idea 97 closeness of EASTNORTHEAST (idea 97: window 5 on one listed tableau, 2% of shuffles).
BERLINCLOCK does not share it (w = 5, p = 0.14), and neither do the full 24 letters. A model where only the first block
carries a small-shift key on a mixed plain alphabet is listed as a follow-up.

**Scope.** This is a statistic, not a decryption. It covers monoalphabetic tableau layouts only (one key value per
letter), no transposition, and the clue positions as given. Solution counts at the smallest w are capped at 10,000 and
counted up to shifting the alphabet. The whole run takes 11 s: 160,116 exact decisions, counted as
text × layout × part × w × window start.
