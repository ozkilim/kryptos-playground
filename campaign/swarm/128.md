# Idea 128: two-word keys on one hidden alphabet

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No two-word key gives readable text. Quagmire III with C = P + K or C = P - K cannot
work at any period from 4 to 26, whatever the key.

## The idea

Scheidt said the key is remembered "key word/s". Idea 93 closed single known words on a fully hidden alphabet,
but not two words written one after the other (SHADOW + FORCES). On a one-alphabet tableau the clue letters put
conditions on the key alone: some pairs of clue places must have the same key letter (S->S with K->K, R->P twice),
others must differ. The numeric model has a few fixed dot products of the key values. Each condition touches one
or two key slots, and each slot belongs to word A or word B. So every word reduces to the letters it puts on
those slots. The two sides are joined by hash and bit masks, and only joined pairs are solved exactly with idea 93's
solver.

## What was run

`attacks/128_two_word_keys_hidden_tableau.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea128_pairs.py`. There are 4,987 cells (one cell is a
form, length, split and phase): A+B at L 4-26 and A+B+A at 2a+b <= 26. Models: true Quagmire III (P + K, P - K,
K - P), true Quagmire II, and the numeric one-alphabet model (repeating and sliding, key values on A-Z or KRYPTOS).
Each cell uses the biggest word-list tier whose join fits under a cap (15,000 pairs located, 4,000 numeric):
big x big (264,828), big x common, common x common (37,896), then theme x common. The run took 10 minutes.

## Numbers

| | survivors | readable | best reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| K4 (all 4,987 cells; 21.35M exact solves) | 22 (all numeric) | 0 | -7.43 |
| shuffled #1 (1/6 of cells) | 0 | 0 | - |
| shuffled #2 (1/6) | 2 | 0 | -7.87 |
| shuffled #3 (1/6) | 0 | 0 | - |

K4's survivors have junk keys (MPE+AQHOUSE, TYSEV+OUTLOOK, RNERANDT+LCSINVERT) and read as gibberish.
**Planted: 5/5.** SHADOW+FORCES, BERLIN+WALL, EAST+BERLIN+EAST, CLOCK+WISE (sliding) and LAYER+TWO+LAYER were each
found among 3,000 decoys, with their alphabets recovered. Each read back 73-88 of 97 letters, scoring -4.4 to -5.3.

## Surprising

For K4, a forced-different pair of clue places always falls on the same key slot for every period 4-26 in true
Quagmire III, C = P + K and P - K. The distances between such places are 1-11 and 31-52, which cover a multiple of every
L up to 26. So that model with any repeating key, words or not, needs period 27-30, 41 or 53+. For shuffled
K4s this does not happen. C = K - P is open only at 18, 23 and 26, and Quagmire II at 8, 11-13, 16, 18-20, 22-24 and 26.

## Scope limits

* The caps mean many cells were covered only for pairs that include a theme word: 1,710 Quagmire II cells
  and 433 Beaufort-type cells. 50 cells were skipped outright (Quagmire II at L 16/19/23, C = K - P at L 23).
* The sliding form was run for the numeric models only. Three or more words, other lists, German and two alphabets
  were not tested, and nothing past L 26.
* The controls ran on a sixth of the cells each. That is why K4's 22 raw survivors outnumber the controls' 2, and neither reads.
