# Idea 100: long-lag plaintext autokey, decided by chains

**Verdict: dead in scope.** No sign that K4 is a plaintext autokey looking 9 to 33 letters back or ahead on a
keyword tableau.

## The idea

Rows of 21 (Bean's vertical pairs, both clues start a row of 21) or 14, each keyed by the row above, is a plaintext
autokey at a long lag: `key[i] = sign * number(plaintext letter L back, or L ahead) + c`, Vigenere or Beaufort form.
Ideas 90 and 96 said plain feed at lags 9-33 cannot be decided, because few or no clue letters sit one lag apart.
That is wrong. With the tableau known, a plaintext letter and the cipher letter one lag on give the next plaintext
letter, so each clue letter fixes its whole class of positions mod L. A class holding two clue letters gives an exact
check.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| checks | 15 | 14 | 13 | 12 | 11 | 11 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 11 | 9 | 7 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

(Lag 18 has 7 checks, not fewer as the idea text said; it was run with the exact lags.)

## What was run

`attacks/100_long_lag_autokey_chains.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea100_chains.py`. 666,993 alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS,
every dictionary keyword alphabet in plain, `~cols` and `~keyed` form) x 5 layouts x Vigenere/Beaufort x fed letter
numbered in M, A-Z or KRYPTOS x added or subtracted x look-back/look-ahead x lags 9-33 x constant c:
**48.0 billion settings**, 22 minutes.

* **Lags 9-23:** every c, all checks required. c is solved from the first chain, not looped.
* **Lags 24-33:** a setting that passes its few checks forces 58-86 letters. A cheap gate reads the two runs forced
  next to the clues (12 and 11 letters); what passes is decrypted in full and scored on the big English model.
  Lags 27-29 have no check: all 26 values of c on the 220,318 plain keyword alphabets, c = 0 only on the 446,675
  transposition-mixed ones (the cut the plan allowed).
* Every survivor of the fast test was re-checked with a separate slow implementation of the model.

## Pre-filter

FLAG on the K5 clue, as for every key that follows the message. Reason to test anyway: the test is exact, and the
stated scope limit of ideas 90 and 96 was wrong.

## Scope limits

* Dictionary keyword alphabets only; no hidden alphabet. Fed letter times +-1 plus one constant only.
* No repeating word on top of the autokey. Clue blocks in place. Plain feed only (cipher feed: ideas 90, 96).
* Lags 27-29 on the mixed alphabets: c = 0 only.
* Lags 24-33 are decided by reading English, not by exact checks. A plaintext that is not ordinary English next to
  the clues would be missed there.
* Controls saw a third of the alphabets each.

## Surprises

When the fed letter is numbered like the plaintext and the key is symmetric in the two letters (Vigenere with +,
Beaufort with -), a two-step chain does not depend on c at all. At lags 18-23 such a view passes for all 26 values
of c or for none; the readings then differ in every second letter of a class. It did not occur on K4. It also
suggests a follow-up: with one hidden alphabet these chains become constant-free linear equations.
