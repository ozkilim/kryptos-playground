# Idea 85: K4 letters as skip counts through the sculpture's own text

**Verdict: dead in scope.** K4's best is 11 of 24 clue letters, 20 shuffled K4s give 9 to 11, and 24 are needed.

## The idea

Sanborn said no book is needed, "just to be able to read", and "who says it is even a math solution?".
So read K4 as distances. The solver keeps a finger on a text that is already on the sculpture. Each K4
letter is a number (A=1 .. Z=26). Count that many letters on; the letter you land on is the plaintext.
No earlier test used the cipher letter as a distance.

## What was run

`attacks/85_skip_counts_through_the_text.py`; the whole run (K4, 20 controls, 7 planted) took 133 seconds.

* **Rule A (moving window):** `P_i = T[s + a*i + d(C_i)]`. The window moves on `a` letters per K4 letter.
* **Rule B (walk):** `q_i = q_(i-1) + a + d(C_i)`, `P_i = T[q_i]`. `a = 1` is the plain A=1..Z=26 walk.
* **Texts (39):** K1, K2 (carved and corrected), K3 plaintext; K1-K3 joined (carved, corrected, with `?`
  kept, with K4 appended); K1-K3 ciphertext; the cipher panel (with and without `?` cells); the tableau
  panel (as carved, body only, body with labels); the Morse text; both panels joined; and the two panels
  read by columns, back-and-forth and along diagonals. Each forwards and backwards, as a ring and as a line.
* **Counts:** place of the letter in A-Z or in the KRYPTOS alphabet, counted forwards or backwards, every
  start, every advance `a` = 0..31. Numbering from 0 or from 1 is inside this (it moves the start or `a`).
* Every setting fixes all 97 letters, so the test is exact: count the clue letters that come out right.

## The surprise: the method can hardly encode anything

A count of at most 26 letters must reach the next plaintext letter. On real text it usually cannot.

* K1-K3 plaintext and the cipher panel: **0 of 200** ordinary English messages could be encoded, by either
  rule, from any start, for any advance 0..31. A walk through K1-K3 dies after about 18 letters (half of
  the messages); through the cipher panel after about 13. K1-K3 has no J or Z, two Qs and four Ks.
* Tableau: rule A works only when the window moves one row per letter or stays still (about 20% of starts
  at `a` = 0; under 1% averaged over `a`). That is an ordinary tableau lookup with a sliding key, a family
  already tested many times. A walk through the tableau works for 2.5-3% of messages at its best advance.
* So the encoder's "choice among occurrences" hardly exists: on the tableau a 26-letter window never
  holds the same letter twice, so nearest and random choice give the same cipher text.
* Index of coincidence where encoding is possible (200 messages each; K4 is 0.0361): rule A on the tableau
  with `a` = 31: mean 0.0385, 41 of 200 at or below K4. Rule A with `a` = 0: mean 0.070, 0 of 200.
  Rule B on the tableau: mean 0.040-0.041, 17-21 of 200 at or below K4. The flat counts are no evidence
  either way.
* For the two K1-K3 planted walks the message had to be a stretch of K1-K3 itself, because no other
  English could be walked through it. That limit is the method's, not the search's.

## Pre-filter

Rule A passes the clue pre-filter (matches in place, same words give the same letters in K5, S can give S).
Rule B is FLAGGED: a walk has state, so the shared words of K5 would not encrypt the same way (3% of
message pairs). It was run anyway because the test is exact and cheap.

## Scope limits

Dead only for the texts listed, as transcribed in this repository, with one cipher letter = one count of at
most 26 letters plus a fixed advance of 0..31. Not covered: texts that are not on the sculpture, counting
words or lines instead of letters, counts made of two letters, counts that skip certain cells, nulls, any
transposition before or after, other numberings of the alphabet. The Morse text is the transcription used
in attack 58.

## Follow-ups

1. Count **words** instead of letters through K1-K3 and take the first letter of the word reached. Unlike
   letter counting, this can encode English, and it fits "just to be able to read" at least as well.
2. Grid lookups into the carved panels with a non-linear row order (the only workable corner of rule A is
   a row-per-letter lookup on the tableau).
