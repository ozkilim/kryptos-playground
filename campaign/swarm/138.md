# Idea 138: the open Quagmire I / II / IV lengths, decided for every alphabet

**Verdict: dead-in-scope.** Two of the 22 open cases close for every alphabet: Quagmire I at key length 20 and
Quagmire II at 13. The other 20 have real solutions with all places distinct, so they stay open for an English test.

## The idea

keithadler's arbitrary-alphabet search left these cases undecided: Quagmire I and II at 13, 16, 19, 20, 23, 24, 26,
and Quagmire IV at 8, 13, 16, 19, 20, 23, 24, 26. Two clue letters in the same residue class mod L share a key
value, so each pair gives one linear equation. The idea plan said "no constant", but that holds only for Quagmire
IV. In Quagmire I and II the straight side is A-Z, so its places are known numbers:
x[P_a] - x[P_b] = C_a - C_b. The solver handles the affine equations directly. For I, II and IV the Beaufort form
is the Vigenere form with one keyed alphabet reversed. Both forms were run, and they agree everywhere.

## What was run

`attacks/138_quagmire_open_lengths.py`, solver in `kryptos/idea138_quag.py`. Each system is decided two ways. The
first is a linear certificate mod 2 and mod 13: either the system is inconsistent, or two letters of one alphabet
are forced to the same place. The second is complete backtracking with distinct places. Every SAT witness is
checked by building the full alphabets and re-deriving a consistent key. Quagmire III was run as a check against
idea 130 and matches it exactly. The run covered L = 1-26 on K4 and on 50 shuffled K4s, and took 6 minutes.

## Numbers

* **Quagmire I, L = 20:** pair 26/66 gives x[O] - x[L] = Q - V = -5, and pair 31/71 gives x[A] - x[O] = K - F = +5.
  So x[A] = x[L]: no alphabet exists, whatever the key.
* **Quagmire II, L = 13:** seven pairs chain y[R] = y[N]+17 = ... = y[K], so y[K] = y[R].
* **Satisfiable on K4:** QI at 13, 16, 19, 23, 24, 26; QII at 16, 19, 20, 23, 24, 26; QIV at all eight open lengths.
  Every UNSAT case for K4 has a linear certificate. The search never had to prove one alone.
* **Controls (50 shuffled K4s):** QI at 20 is closed for 42/50 shuffles, and QII at 13 for 28/50, so these closures
  are ordinary. Over L = 1-26 the number of closed lengths is QI: K4 20 vs shuffled median 21; QII: 20 vs 19;
  QIV: 18 vs 15 (8/50 shuffles ≥ K4). One of 4,290 control systems ran out of budget.
* **Planted: 22/22**, one per model and open length, with random alphabets and keys in both forms. All SAT, with no
  false certificate, and all read back.

## Scope limits

This decides consistency of the 24 clue letters only, not English. It covers only a plain repeating key under the
ACA definitions, with the carved clue positions and no transposition. The 20 satisfiable cases fix very few places
(1-16 equations), so very many alphabets fit, and only a hill-climb on English can judge them.

## Surprising

L = 19 kills almost every shuffled K4 (only 1-2 of 50 survive for each model), but K4 survives. The reason is that
positions 27 and 65 are both R->P, 38 = 2 x 19 apart, which turns one equation into 0 = 0. This comes from how the
clues are arranged and says nothing about the cipher. Even so, L = 19 is the tightest open case left for a
constrained English search.
