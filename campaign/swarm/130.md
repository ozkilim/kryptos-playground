# Idea 130: Quagmire III, Beaufort form, key length 18, for every alphabet

**Verdict: dead.** No alphabet of any kind fits K4 at key length 18. The proof takes two lines.

## The idea

K1 and K2 are Quagmire III: one keyed alphabet on both sides of the tableau. Write x[ch] for a letter's place in
that unknown alphabet. The Beaufort form says key = x[C] + x[P] mod 26. Two clue letters in the same residue class
mod L share a key value, so each pair gives one linear equation. The community's exact search left lengths 13, 18,
23 and 26 open. Idea 86 closed 13, but 18 only for keyword alphabets of up to 16 letters. Pre-filter: PASS.

## What was run

`attacks/130_quagmire3_beaufort_18.py`, solver in `kryptos/idea130_exact.py`. Each system is decided two
independent ways:

* **Certificate:** linear algebra mod 2 and mod 13. If the equations force two different letters to the same place,
  no alphabet exists.
* **Search:** complete backtracking over the letters in the equations, all places different. The gauge is fixed
  (one letter at 0, the next at 1, 2 or 13).

The two never disagreed. Four Vigenere systems at lengths 24-48 ran out of search budget; the certificate decided them.

## Numbers

At L = 18 there are 7 equations on 17 letters. Two of them are:

    class 9:  pos 27 (C=P, P=R), pos 63 (C=N, P=B):  x[P] + x[R] = x[N] + x[B]
    class 11: pos 29 (C=N, P=H), pos 65 (C=P, P=R):  x[N] + x[H] = x[P] + x[R]

So x[B] = x[H], which is impossible. This holds whatever the key is. The search agrees (UNSAT, 1,951 steps). The
same two classes meet whenever L divides 36, so **L = 36 is closed too**.

Across lengths 1-26 the solver reproduces the published results exactly. The Beaufort form is empty at 1-22, 24
and 25; length 13 is closed by T = H. It is satisfiable at 23 (100,000 or more solutions) and at 26 (one equation).
The Vigenere form is empty at all 26 lengths. Every empty case has a short certificate.

**Controls (50 shuffled K4s):** at L = 18, **31/50 also have no alphabet**, and 19 have one. So being closed at 18
is ordinary and not a signal. At L = 23 the split was 24 SAT and 26 UNSAT; at L = 26 it was 45 SAT and 5 UNSAT.
Over lengths 1-26, K4 is closed at 24. The shuffled K4s were closed at 16-24 (median 21), and 3/50 matched K4's 24.
K4's repeated clue pairs (R->P twice) and fixed points make it close a little more often than random text.

**Planted: 13/13.** Ten were at L = 18 and one each at 13, 23 and 26, all with a random alphabet, a random key and
English carrying K4's clue words. Every one came out SAT. With 5 equation letters freed, the true alphabet was
listed (among 1 to 624). Its reading ranked first (-4.05 to -4.98) and gave back the plaintext.

## Scope limits

* Only Quagmire III with a plain repeating key and the carved clue positions.
* Not covered: Quagmire I, II or IV at 18, other key rules, transposition layers, gap slack.
* At 23 and 26 the solutions were counted, not read. The clues don't fix the rest of the alphabet, so English
  hill-climbing is the only way to judge them.
* Lengths 27-48 on K4 are a by-product with no controls.

## Surprising

* The community listed 18 as unresolved, but it falls to a single pair of equations.
* Plain linear algebra (two letters forced equal) decides every closed case in well under a second. No permutation
  search was ever needed to prove a length empty.
