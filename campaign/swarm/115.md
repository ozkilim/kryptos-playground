# Idea 115: two sculpture texts added together as the key

**Verdict: dead in scope.** Nothing came up on K4 or on any of the 5 shuffled controls. All 6 planted answers were found.

**The idea.** K2 ends with LAYER TWO. Using one sculpture text as a running key is already dead (best 10 of 24). Two stacked keys had only been closed for repeating words. Here two running texts are laid over each other, `K[i] = T1[a+i] ± T2[b+i] + c`. That key never repeats, but the 24 clue letters decide it exactly.

**What was run** (`attacks/115_two_sculpture_texts.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea115_twotexts.py`):

* **Texts.** 20 in all: K1-K3 plaintext (carved and corrected), K1-K3 ciphertext (carved, and with K2 corrected), the cipher and tableau panels read by rows and by columns, the Morse text and K4. Each is read forwards and backwards, from every start, with letters numbered on A-Z or KRYPTOS.
- **The constant c.** It is left free, which covers numbering from 0 or 1 and any shift of the tableau. It costs one check.
* **Forced key.** Taken in four views (C-P, P-C, C+P, -C-P), on the A-Z and KRYPTOS tableaux and on 84 theme alphabets (1,708 key vectors).
* **Part A: the two-text join.** Every 13-letter (block 1) and 11-letter (block 2) window goes into a hash table. Each first-text window is subtracted from the forced key and the rest is looked up. Every block hit is then checked on the other block, at the true gap and with the gap shifted up to ±30.
* **Part B: one text plus a word.** The word repeats (length 1-10), or slides (a running sum of a word of length 1-10). A third model uses the running sum of a text plus a repeating word. These tests are linear too, so they are exact hash joins.

**Numbers for K4:**

| Test | Checks | Hits | Expected by chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block 1 alone | 12 | 0 | 2.5e-5 |
| Block 2 alone | 10 | 0 | 0.017 |
| Both blocks at the true gap | 23 | 0 | 6e-21 |
| Part B (1.69e9 window tests) | | 0 | 2e-11 |

* Each block-alone test covers 2.4e12 window pairs.
* The closest near misses were 10 of 13 letters in block 1 and 9 of 11 in block 2.

**Controls.** 5 shuffled K4s, same search: 0 hits in every part.

- Their closest near misses were also 10 of 13 and 9 of 11. One shuffle reached 10 of 11 in block 2.
- The near-miss counts are the same size as K4's, so K4 does not stand out.

**Planted answers: 6/6.** Each came back as the only reading and decrypted all 97 of 97 letters:

1. K3 plaintext minus K3 ciphertext at two offsets, on the KRYPTOS tableau.
2. A K2 line plus a tableau column, on A-Z with Beaufort.
3. The tableau rows backwards minus K4 backwards, on the PALIMPSEST alphabet.
4. The cipher panel columns plus SHADOW.
5. The running sum of the K1-K3 plaintext plus LAYER.
6. The K1-K3 ciphertext backwards plus a sliding BERLIN.

The pre-filter passes. `tests/test_idea_115.py` runs plants 1, 2 and 4 in about 8 s. The full run took about 2 minutes, so the scope could be widened cheaply.

**Scope limits.** This does not rule out any of the following:

* texts from off the sculpture;
* other ways of walking the panels (diagonals, every d-th letter);
* text letters numbered on the keyed alphabet itself (a true Quagmire key lookup);
* dictionary or hidden alphabets;
* multipliers, or a third text;
* words longer than 10 letters in Part B.

**Surprising.** Nothing turned up. The near-miss histograms carry fat tails, much larger than uniform letters would give, because the tableau streams are so regular. K4 and the controls show the same tails, so they are not a signal.
