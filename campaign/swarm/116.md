# Idea 116: free choice of system per letter (retry of 87)

**Verdict: duplicate.** Nothing was re-run.

## The idea

There is one repeating (or sliding) key word. At each letter the encoder may use either of two or three systems:
Vigenere, Beaufort or variant on one tableau, or one form on two tableaux. The point is to flatten the counts. The
reader tries each system and keeps whichever reads. Idea 116 was queued because idea 87 was thought to have crashed
at start-up and never run.

## Why it is a duplicate

That premise is out of date. The crash was in an early campaign run. Idea 87 was later run in full in the swarm:
`attacks/87_free_choice_per_letter.py` with `kryptos/idea87_freeform.py`, commit f31f5a9, ledger line 304, card
`campaign/swarm/87.json`. Its plan is the plan of 116, item by item:

* the same choice sets: {V,B}, {V,var}, {B,var}, all three, and M against A-Z or KRYPTOS;
* the same alphabets: A-Z, KRYPTOS and 220,316 dictionary keyword alphabets in the five layouts of idea 66;
* the same key lengths: repeating L 1-17, then the sliding key;
* the same strength measure (-log26 of the chance pass rate) with a bar of 7, and chance passes printed beside counts;
* the same beam-search reading, 3 shuffled K4s on a third of the alphabets each, and planted keys of 7 and 11 letters
  (LANTERN, UNDERGROUND) with a coin flip per letter.

Its result: K4 had 0 of 167,589,803 settings at strength 7+ (chance bound 0.0004) and 0 at 5-7. The controls had
0 and 0 in all three. Planted answers: 4/4 found and read back 90-98%. I re-ran `tests/test_idea_87.py` in this
tree: 3/3 pass in 1.8 seconds, so the code still works.

## Scope limits (idea 87's, unchanged)

The question is decided only for short keys: 2-way choice up to L 11 (repeating) and 10 (sliding), 3-way up to 9 and
8, and 4-way up to 7. L 12-17 were run but are too weak to decide, and L above 17 was not run. Only keyword alphabets
were used. The open follow-ups are 87's: the same set test on transposition-mixed and non-keyword alphabets, and a
count-flattening choice rule, which would give an exact test.
