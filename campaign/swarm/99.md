# Idea 99: half-hidden Quagmire IV (one dictionary alphabet, the other anything)

**Verdict: dead in scope.** 0 settings pass with 5 or more checks; K4's 5 weak survivors read as noise.

## The idea

Sanborn said in 2005 that he changed the system "so that Scheidt could not decipher it"; Scheidt speaks of "key
word/s". Quagmire IV uses two mixed alphabets and a short key. Idea 63 closed two dictionary alphabets, idea 20 a
hidden alphabet against A-Z or KRYPTOS, idea 86 an unknown keyword alphabet. This run tests the case between: one
alphabet from a dictionary word, the other written down freely, with a short key of unknown numbers.

## What was run

`attacks/99_half_hidden_quagmire4.py`, helpers in `kryptos/idea99_half.py`, test `tests/test_idea_99.py`.

* **Known alphabet:** 666,993 alphabets (A-Z, KRYPTOS, every dictionary keyword alphabet and its two
  transposition-mixed forms), on the plain side with the cipher alphabet hidden (case 1) and on the cipher side with
  the plain alphabet hidden (case 2).
* **Key:** repeating, 1-12 unknown numbers; or sliding (the running sum of an unknown word) for words of 1-9 letters,
  all 25 word sums. The idea asked for 1-9 and 1-6; the rest cost nothing to scan.
* **Test:** exact. Hidden letters and key slots are nodes, each of the 24 clue letters is an edge, every closed loop
  is one check on the known alphabet. A setting survives only if all loops close and the hidden letters sit on
  different places. Vigenere and Beaufort form are the same test (turn the hidden alphabet back to front), so each
  setting is tested once. 474 settings per alphabet, 316,154,682 in all, 24 minutes with controls and plants.
* **Survivors** are completed by simulated annealing on the big English model (quick pass for all, 6 x 20,000 steps
  for the best 20).
* The clue pre-filter passes the encryptor (score 1.0, no fact or firm statement broken).

**K4.** All 5 survivors have the cipher alphabet hidden and key length 8: 2 with a repeating key (4 checks; chance

readings score -6.11, -6.27, -6.31, -6.46, -6.52, word coverage 0.30-0.37. Two of the three shuffled texts read
better than that.

**Controls.** Three shuffled K4s, each on all alphabets. None has a pass with 5+ checks. Their survivor counts vary a
lot (2 to 15,535) because a shuffled text is often not blocked at lengths 10 and 12 the way K4 is; none of the
readings is English.

**Planted.** Nine English messages carrying the real clue words, each under a random dictionary alphabet (hidden among
166,749 others) and a random 26-letter alphabet: repeating keys CLUE, LANTERN, UNDERGROUND (cipher side hidden),
LIGHT, INTELLIGENCE, BERLIN in Beaufort form (plain side hidden), sliding keys KEY, DUSK, MAGNETIC (Beaufort). All
nine were found with the right alphabet named and read back, 82-97 of 97 letters, -4.19 to -4.76. The best wrong
reading among up to 2,000 other survivors was -5.28 to -6.06.

## Surprising

The idea expected 11 - L checks to do the work. They do something stronger: for K4 most of them do not depend on the
dictionary at all. With a repeating key, 19 of the 24 (side, length) cells are impossible for **any** known alphabet,
because repeated clue letters cancel and leave "two different letters on one place" (for example at length 12, R gives
P in slots 3 and 5, so the two slots have the same shift, yet N comes from H in one and from B in the other). Cipher
side hidden: every length 1-12 except 8. Plain side hidden: 1-7 and 9 by the loops; at 10, 11 and 12 some alphabets
close the loops (362, 800, 1,332) but all put two hidden letters on one place. This agrees with the community result
that general Quagmire IV is out at 1-7, 9, 10 and adds 11 and 12. So key length 8 is the only short repeating key
where the dictionary mattered, and there chance alone gives about 0.15 survivors (K4 has 2, unreadable).

## Scope limits

* Known alphabet: only the 666,993 listed. Decimated or reversed dictionary alphabets, keywords outside the dictionary
  and both alphabets hidden are not covered. Both hidden at key length 8 stays open.
* Sliding keys are decided well for words of 3-7 letters (4+ checks). At 8 and 9 there are 2-3 checks: the 3 survivors
  at 8 were read and are noise; at 9 (cipher side hidden) 961 alphabets close the loops and all collide.
* Key lengths 13 and up are not run (0-1 checks, tens of thousands of chance fits).
* Shuffled-text survivors beyond the best 20 got only the quick annealing pass, which can miss a weakly forced
  long-key system (a planted 11-letter key with 0 checks needed the long pass). K4's 5 survivors all got the long pass.
* The "chance expects" figures assume a random known alphabet; they are a guide, the shuffled texts are the gauge.
