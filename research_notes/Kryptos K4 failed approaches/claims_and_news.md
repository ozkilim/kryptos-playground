# Kryptos K4: rejected claimed solutions, and the 2025-2026 news (Smithsonian find, RR Auction, Paradigm, the $1 checker, Kryptos CTF), read for what K4 is NOT

Research date: 2026-11-15. Ciphertext and cribs as given in the brief (EASTNORTHEAST at 22-34, BERLINCLOCK at 64-74, 1-indexed). These notes do not reproduce or guess the K4 plaintext. The plaintext found in the Smithsonian has not been published by anyone who saw it.

Confidence tags: [HIGH] = primary source or several reliable outlets agree; [MED] = one reliable outlet, or a primary source with ambiguous wording; [LOW/RUMOUR] = blog, aggregator, social post or unverified.

## Q1. Which high-profile claimed solutions were rejected, and why?

### Takeaway
Reliable media name almost no individual K4 claimants. What is documented is a pattern: a growing flood of solutions written with AI tools, especially since early 2025 (Grok 3 and ChatGPT are named), all rejected by Sanborn. Every claim so far has failed one of three tests: it lacks the confirmed cribs at the right positions; its method cannot be reproduced by someone else; or it fits the cribs only because the key was worked backwards from them. Since June 2026 there is also an objective test, Paradigm's $1 hash checker, and no claim has publicly passed it.

**Community acceptance criteria (why claims get rejected)**
- Elonka Dunin's Kryptos FAQ (last modified 2020-07-13) sets two requirements for credit:

  - a method explained well enough for an independent third party to get the same result.

**Named or high-profile "solutions" that were disputed**
- **Kobek and Byrne, Sept-Oct 2025.** This is the most prominent "solution," though it was not a decryption.
  - They found the plaintext in the Smithsonian's Archives of American Art.
  - Sanborn confirmed the text was accurate but rejected the word "solved." His words: "K4 has not been solved. K4 has been discovered."
  - Kobek: "There's no way on earth that this is a cryptographic solve." [HIGH] — [RR Auction blog, 2025-10-23](https://content.rrauction.com/kryptos-k4-discovered-not-solved-heres-what-actually-happened/); [Zona Motel interview with Kobek, 2025-10-17](https://zonamotel.substack.com/p/interview-kryptos-k4-uncovered-jarett)

- **SolveKryptos.com / Matt Lacy, June-July 2026.** A public "reconstructed" 97-letter plaintext plus a mechanism: a Quagmire III variant with a helper keystream read from the back of the sculpture and a one-bit gate fixed by position.
  - The site admits only about 24 of 97 positions are confirmed.
  - It also admits most key values are "back-solved from the plaintext," so the fit shows internal consistency, not proof.
  - It says "No one has solved K4 cryptographically" and does not claim to have passed Paradigm's checker. [MED: primary claimant page; treat as an unverified claim] — [SolveKryptos, "K4 Solution: Reconstructed Plaintext"](https://solvekryptos.com/solution)
- **ElodineOfficial/KryptosK4 (GitHub).** Reports a partial result said to cover half of the known crib words. It is not presented as a full solution. [LOW] — [GitHub ElodineOfficial/KryptosK4](https://github.com/ElodineOfficial/KryptosK4)
- **2006 K2 correction.** Not a K4 claim, but relevant: Sanborn disclosed that a missing X in the K2 plaintext had led solvers to a wrong K2 ending. Published "solutions" of the sculpture have been wrong before. [HIGH] — [Wikipedia, Kryptos (citing Wired 2006-04-20)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryptos)

### Inferences
- **Failure pattern 1: crib mismatch.** Chatbot "solutions" usually produce fluent English that does not put EASTNORTHEAST at 22-34 and BERLINCLOCK at 64-74. For the negatives DB: any candidate must reproduce all 24 crib positions exactly, or it is rejected immediately.
- **Failure pattern 2: back-solved keys.** With a free per-position key (running key, a long Quagmire key, a "keystream read from the sculpture"), any plaintext can be made to fit the cribs. That fit is not evidence. A real solution needs a key that is short or generated independently, and that produces the 73 non-crib letters without tuning. SolveKryptos is the clearest public example of this pattern.
- **Failure pattern 3: method with no reproducible plaintext.** This is the failure the FAQ describes.
- **Since June 2026, failure is objective.** Any full 97-letter candidate can be tested for $1. No claim has publicly passed.

### Gaps
- No reliable outlet (NYT, Wired, WaPo, SciAm) names specific individual K4 claimants rejected by Sanborn's email replies. I found no copies of Sanborn's standard rejection emails. Reddit r/KryptosK4 threads exist but were not reliable or fetchable enough to cite.
- I could not fetch the original Wired articles (March 2025, Aug 2025, June 2026) directly; wired.com blocks the fetcher. Details come from Gizmodo, TheNote and GeekBlog reprints.
- No public list of claims that were run through Paradigm's checker and failed.

## Q2. What did the Smithsonian and auction materials disclose about K4's method or plaintext structure?

### Takeaway
The plaintext was found, but only as physical, hand-made scraps. The method was not found, and the sources agree that neither the finders nor RR Auction know it. Since the find, Sanborn has released a few new non-crib hints:
- the 1986 Egypt trip and the 1989 fall of the Berlin Wall both figure in the solution;
- BERLINCLOCK means the World Clock at Alexanderplatz;
- the theme is "delivering a message";
- "Who says it is even a math solution?";
- K5 is 97 letters and shares some coded words with K4 in the same positions.

The RR lot shows that K4 was made with a "coding system" plus "coding charts," with hand-written plaintexts used for screen cutting. That points to a hand process, but none of this is described publicly.

### Inferences
- **The plaintext predates 1990 and was handled by hand.** Plaintext strips existed in 1990, and there were hand-written plaintexts for screen cutting plus "coding charts." So the plaintext was fixed before the sculpture was cut and the encryption was driven by paper charts. That argues against computer-only constructions such as modern stream ciphers or hashes. [Inference, MED]
- **K5 shares K4's key schedule.** "Coded words in the same positions" suggests K4 and K5 use the same position-dependent key, so the same plaintext word at the same index gives the same ciphertext. Two consequences:
  - if K5 is released, a K4/K5 depth (two-time-pad-style) attack becomes possible;
  - K4's key probably depends on position rather than on earlier plaintext or ciphertext (as an autokey would). If the key depended on earlier text, shared words at shared positions would almost never encrypt identically. [Inference, MED-LOW: this depends on reading "coded words" as identical ciphertext segments]
- **K4 is not a pure arithmetic cipher.** "Who says it is even a math solution?" plus "creativity" suggests a physical, positional or artistic step (for example, something read off the sculpture, the site, or the Weltzeituhr). This is weak evidence against purely algebraic ciphers such as Hill. [Inference, LOW]
- **K4 is a separate system from K1-K3.** The lot lists K4's coding system separately from the K1-K3 charts, which fits Scheidt's statement that the methodology was deliberately changed (see Q4). So a plain reuse of the K1/K2 Vigenère tableau or the K3 transposition is unlikely. That matches decades of failed attempts at exactly that. [Inference, MED]

### Gaps
- No public description exists of the coding charts' format (tableau, grid, matrix, pages). Neither the RR listing nor any news piece describes them.
- The NYT 2025-10-16 article could not be fetched (paywall/block); details are via Wikipedia's summary.
- What exactly Kobek and Byrne saw of the coding charts at the Smithsonian is unclear. Sources say they found plaintext scraps; none says they saw a coding chart.

## Q3. What has Paradigm said about K4 and the checker? Are there statistics on attempts?

### Takeaway
On 2026-06-12, the venture firm Paradigm (Dan Robinson, Matt Huang) revealed it was the anonymous November 2025 buyer. It launched paradigm.xyz/kryptos with a blind hash-based K4 verifier ($1 per guess) and a ten-puzzle CTF. It says it keeps the answer secret even from its own staff and plans to release K5 "in the future." I found no published count of checker submissions.

### Cited Findings
- **Ownership.** Paradigm disclosed in June 2026 that it was the anonymous winning bidder. [HIGH] — [Wikipedia, citing NYT 2026-06-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryptos); [NYT 2026-06-12 (paywalled, not fetched)](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/12/science/kryptos-sale-cryptography.html)
  - "We're honored to announce that we are the new stewards of the Kryptos secret." [HIGH: primary] — [Paradigm, "Project Kryptos", 2026-06-12, Dan Robinson & Matt Huang](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/kryptos)
  - The About page says Paradigm became steward in November 2025 and keeps the answer secret "even from ourselves." It describes K4 as unsolved after "thousands of attempts." [HIGH] — [Paradigm, About Kryptos](https://paradigm.xyz/kryptos/about)
- **Checker design** (primary): [HIGH] — [Paradigm, 2026-06-12](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/kryptos)
  - Sanborn entered the plaintext on a new, dedicated computer.
  - A program computed its SHA-256 hash and sent it to Google Cloud KMS, which returned an HMAC tag. Only the same hash sent to the same key reproduces the tag.
  - The plaintext "never left the device," and the laptop was wiped afterward.
  - The verifier checks submissions against the solution Sanborn provided.
  - Each guess costs $1, to deter brute force.
  - The post uses "Jim" ambiguously when describing who put the archive up for auction. It was Sanborn's archive sold through RR Auction, not Gillogly's.
- **Fee in the rules.** The official rules confirm K4 submissions may require a $1 fee. The ten CTF puzzles have no entry fee. [HIGH] — [Paradigm Kryptos CTF official rules](https://paradigm.xyz/kryptos-ctf/official-rules); [Rules](https://paradigm.xyz/kryptos-ctf/rules)
- **Sealed envelopes.** Paradigm reportedly confirmed in June 2026 that it had not opened the sealed envelopes holding the K4 plaintext, encryption system and key. [LOW: single blog, no primary link] — [Intel Today, 2026-11-03](https://inteltoday.org/2026/11/03/on-this-day-kryptos-sculpture-dedication-ceremony-at-the-cia-november-3-1990-update-can-ai-solve-kryptos-k4/)
- **K5.** Sanborn created a 97-character K5 ciphertext at the same time as K4. Paradigm plans to release it "in the future," with no date. [HIGH] — [Paradigm, 2026-06-12](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/kryptos)
- **Why the CTF.** There has been "an explosion in AI-assisted attempts" with no success, so the CTF is meant as a feedback loop for building better tools. [HIGH] — [Paradigm, 2026-06-12](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/kryptos)
- **Statistics.** No count of checker submissions or revenue has been published. The only figures are "thousands have tried," "more than 13,000 days," and "more than three decades." [HIGH, as an absence] — [Paradigm, 2026-06-12](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/kryptos)
- **Crypto-buyer speculation (Nov 2025).** Crypto Briefing reported early online speculation that the buyer had crypto ties, before the reveal. That speculation proved right. [MED] — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/kryptos-sculpture-solution-auction/)

### Inferences
- **The checker is exact-match only.** Because it compares a SHA-256 of the exact plaintext, it gives no partial credit. A near-miss (one wrong letter, or a different spacing or normalization) returns "no," so it cannot serve as an oracle for hill-climbing. Each guess costs $1. Before spending, the attack repo should settle the input normalization Paradigm expects: 97 uppercase letters with no spaces is the likely form, but check the site UI. [Inference]
- **No method hints from Paradigm.** Paradigm has not seen the plaintext and published nothing about method. Its materials constrain K4 only through the CTF design choices (Q4).

### Gaps
- No submission counts or pass/fail statistics for the checker.
- The exact input normalization the checker accepts (case, spaces, the 97-letter requirement) is not in the fetched pages.
- The NYT and Wired June 2026 articles could not be fetched directly, so any Robinson or Sanborn quotes they contain are missing here.

## Q4. Are there CTF write-ups for PK1-PK10, and what do insiders say about K4's method?

### Takeaway
At least one complete public write-up of all ten puzzles exists, in Spanish, posted 2026-10-26 (saul-g/kryptos-ctf-2026). Paradigm's puzzles are built almost entirely from:
- Quagmire III over the KRYPTOS-keyed alphabet, single and stacked;
- columnar, double-columnar and spiral transpositions;
- a 3x3 Hill cipher;
- running keys taken from the previous puzzle's plaintext.

These show the puzzle designers' idea of "Kryptos-like" ciphers. They are not information about K4: Paradigm does not know the method. Separately, insiders have said the following about method:
- Scheidt: K4's methodology was intentionally changed.
- Sanborn: "Who says it is even a math solution?"
- Sanborn: "delve into that particular clock" (the Weltzeituhr).
- Kobek: computer cryptanalysis is unlikely to work.

### Cited Findings
**Kryptos CTF facts**
- Format: ten independent puzzles, PK1-PK10, of increasing difficulty. $1,000 to the first solver of each. Open until solved. Launched 2026-06-12. [HIGH] — [Paradigm CTF rules](https://paradigm.xyz/kryptos-ctf/rules); [Paradigm, 2026-06-12](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/kryptos); [Dan Robinson on X](https://x.com/danrobinson/status/2065438501961887785)
- Early progress: a search summary claimed four puzzles fell within hours of launch. [LOW/RUMOUR: no attributable source found]
- Late July 2026: a solver posted that a community effort was aimed at PK8 ("If you make it past PK7, let me know"), implying PK8-PK10 were still open then. [LOW: social post] — [@0xdiid on X](https://x.com/0xdiid/status/2080831626863656972)

### Inferences: what K4 is NOT
1. **Not a cipher where no letter can map to itself.** Plaintext K = ciphertext K at position 74. That rules out Enigma-style reciprocal machines and any scheme with a guaranteed fixed-point-free substitution at every position. [HIGH: derived from a confirmed crib]
2. **Not plain K1/K2 Quagmire III or the K3 route transposition, as-is.** Scheidt says the method was deliberately changed, decades of direct attacks failed, and the RR lot lists K4's coding system separately. The CTF puzzles (Paradigm's guess at "Kryptos-like") lean heavily on stacked Quagmire III plus transposition. Paradigm does not know K4's method, though, so the CTF's design is not evidence that K4 is built this way. Treat the CTF as a tool-validation suite: a K4 attack pipeline should at least solve PK1-PK10. [MED]
3. **Probably not a computer-only cipher.** The 1990 hand-cut plaintext strips, the "coding charts," Sanborn's "not a math solution," Kobek's doubt about computer attacks, and Sanborn saying the method doesn't matter all point to a paper process. That process may include a non-arithmetic or physical step tied to the Weltzeituhr, Egypt 1986 or the Berlin Wall in 1989. [LOW-MED]
4. **Probably not a scheme with plaintext- or ciphertext-dependent keying (e.g. autokey).** This holds if K5's "coded words in the same positions as K4" means identical ciphertext at the same index (see Q2). [LOW-MED]
5. **Not solvable by language models alone.** Every documented AI claim has failed, and Paradigm says none has succeeded. The failures are crib mismatches or back-solved keys. [HIGH as an empirical record]
